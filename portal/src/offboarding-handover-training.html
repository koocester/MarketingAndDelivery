Offboarding& Handover

How we leave well, and hand over cleanly.

Why this matters

How you leave is part of the culture.

A clean exit is not a courtesy. It protects four things at once, and it protects them all in the same move.

Clientsthe relationships and the work in flight stay covered, with no gap they can feel
The teamno scramble, no dropped tasks, no scanning for who used to own this
The knowledgewhat lived in one head gets written down and kept in the company
Reputation, theirs and oursthey speak well of us, and we speak well of them
Leave the door open. How someone leaves is part of who we are, as much as how they arrive.
The lifecycle bookend

Offboarding is the planned counterpart to onboarding.

1
Join
First 90 Days, you arrive and earn trust
2
Contribute
You do the work, own outcomes, raise the bar
3
Leave well
Offboarding, the closing bookend to the start
Not an afterthought. We plan the exit with the same care we plan the first 90 days.
Two tracks · both must happen

The leaver hands over. The manager and HR offboard.

The Leaver

runs the handover
  • Documents everything they own
  • Transfers the knowledge
  • Names a successor for each item

Manager + HR

run the offboarding checklist
  • Close access and security
  • Handle final admin
  • Run the exit interview
Both, in parallel. One track without the other leaves a gap.
The trigger · day one of the exit

The exit starts the day you know.

1
Same day
Manager tells HR the moment someone resigns, or is decided not to continue
2
Agree
Last working day and notice period, set together
3
Handle
Confidential and respectful, from start to finish
Same day, every time. The sooner HR knows, the more room there is to hand over well.
The handover document · the leaver's job

Write down everything you own.

  1. Everything you own, your responsibilities and areas
  2. Work in progress and its current status
  3. Where files live, folders, drives, and docs
  4. Logins, and who should own them next
  5. Client and contact relationships, the people you hold
  6. Recurring tasks and their cadence
Name a successor or owner for every item. Nothing leaves without a new home.
Knowledge transfer · move what is in your head

Overlap, record, then hand over live.

1
Overlap
Shadow where you can, so the successor sees the work live
2
Record
A short screen walkthrough for anything only you know
3
Hand over
A live session with your successor
The goal is simple. Nothing critical leaves the company with you.
Access & security · HR and Tech lane

Close every door on the last day.

  1. Revoke logins across every tool
  2. Transfer ownership of social accounts and drives
  3. Remove from groups, Lark and channels
  4. Redirect or archive email, so nothing is lost
  5. Collect the company device, then wipe or reassign it
Ties to the Security and IT and Company Devices modules. Same standards, applied on the way out.
Final admin · HR and Finance lane

Settle the paperwork cleanly.

  1. Final pay and leave balance, settled correctly
  2. Return of company property, devices, cards, and access items
  3. Any clawbacks, handled per contract
  4. Confidentiality and intellectual property reminder, what stays with the company
  5. References policy, what we will and will not confirm
Clean books, clean exit. HR and Finance close this out together.
The exit interview · always run it

Capture the real reason, and whether we would rejoin them.

The real reason

why they are leaving
  • The honest reason, not the polite one
  • What we could have done better

Would we rejoin them?

the rehire call
  • Yes, no, or with conditions
  • Feeds retention and who we welcome back
Logged in the HR base Exit Interview table. It shapes retention and who we welcome back.
Offboard with dignity
Alumni are future clients, referrers, and rehires.

Most people leave as Good to have again. That is the goal, every time.

Burn a bridge

A cold, rushed exit. They remember it, and so does everyone watching.

Leave it open

A clean, warm exit. They come back as a client, send referrals, or rejoin.

Never burn a bridge. The lean team you build today is the network you draw on tomorrow.
