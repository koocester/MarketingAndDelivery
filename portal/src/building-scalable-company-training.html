Part one

Constrain the system

From The Goal and the Theory of Constraints.

Find the limiting point

The whole business moves at the speed of its constraint.

DemandQualified opportunity

Enough of the right customers enter the system.

→
ConstraintThe limiting stage

The one point where work queues, slows or loses value.

→
ThroughputCash-generating result

Completed value reaches the customer and becomes a sale.

Do not optimise everything. Improve the point that limits the whole system.
The five focusing steps

One constraint. One disciplined cycle.

1Identify

Name the current constraint with evidence.

2Exploit

Get more from it before adding cost.

3Subordinate

Align other work to protect its flow.

4Elevate

Add capability when the earlier moves are not enough.

5Repeat

The constraint moves; find the next one.

Measure business flow

Busyness is not throughput.

ThroughputMoney generated through sales

Value reaches a customer and converts into economic output.

Inventory / work in progressMoney trapped in the system

Unfinished work consumes time and cash without completion.

Operating expenseMoney spent to run the system

Resources required to turn opportunity into delivered value.

Part two

Systemise the work

From Work the System and Get Scalable.

Adoption turns documentation into a system

A system exists only when the team uses it.

01 · DefineName the expected result

State the observable outcome, quality standard, evidence and deadline. Someone new should know what “done well” means.

02 · EmbedPut it where work starts

Place it at the top of the procedure and inside the Lark task or project template, beside the owner and metric.

03 · AdoptProve it in live work

Train on a real case, observe execution, review exceptions weekly and improve the mechanism until it runs without reminders.

Expected result = outcome + quality standard + evidence + deadline.
Work the System · three levels of clarity

Each result has one proper home.

01 · CompanyStrategic objective

The company-level result: what Koocester exists to accomplish and the direction all systems serve.

02 · DecisionsOperating principles

The rules and boundaries people use when the procedure does not cover the situation.

03 · ProcessWorking procedure

Expected result first, then owner, steps, controls and evidence. Link it directly from the Lark work template.

Never make people hunt for the standard. The procedure is the source; the Lark task or project is the point of use.

PPeople

Part three

Make the company transferable

From Built to Sell and Built to Last.

Built to Sell · value beyond the founder

A sellable company is simply a transferable company.

1Specialised

A clear offer for a clear customer and problem.

2Repeatable

Sales and delivery follow a consistent method.

3Independent

Customers, staff and decisions do not rely on the founder.

4Recurring

Revenue is more predictable than one-off projects alone.

Transferability risks

Growth can hide a fragile company.

Concentration risk

  • One customer dominates revenue
  • One person holds critical relationships
  • One channel creates most demand
  • One custom offer consumes delivery

Transferable value

  • Customer mix is deliberate
  • Relationships belong to the company
  • Demand comes from repeatable channels
  • Offers have clear scope, margin and process
Test: could a capable leader operate this company for 90 days without asking the founder how everything works?
Built to Last · know what stays and what moves

Preserve the core. Change the method.

Preserve · identity
Purpose

Why Koocester exists: to be the growth partner businesses trust to deliver outcomes that matter.

Values

GUIDE behaviours that govern hiring, decisions and how we act when pressure makes compromise tempting.

Change · response
Strategy

Where we play and how we win now: markets, offers, priorities and resource bets.

Practice

How work is done now: processes, roles, tools, cadences, templates and controls.

The test: change the method when it improves the result without trading away purpose, trust or GUIDE.
Clock building, not time telling

Build an organisation that keeps making good decisions.

Time tellingThe leader has the answer

Results depend on one person’s judgment, presence and energy. The company waits for the next instruction.

Clock buildingThe organisation produces answers

Purpose, people, mechanisms and information create good decisions across leaders and product cycles.
