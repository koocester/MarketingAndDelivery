Part one

How the areas group

Four groups, so sixteen areas are easy to hold.

Bring people in

Plan the seat. Fill it well.

  1. Workforce planning — know what roles you need before you're desperate for them.
  2. Recruitment & talent acquisition — per the Recruitment & Talent Acquisition SOP: 8 phases, Vacancy Approval → Sourcing → Screening → Interviews → Verification → Offer → Handover. Target ≤45 days to fill, ≥90% offer acceptance. Never issue an offer, and never skip background verification, without approval.
  3. Onboarding — per the Employee Onboarding SOP: 9 phases running from Day One readiness through Week 1 / Day 30 / 60 / 90 reviews. Nail Day One — equipment, access, and workspace ready before they arrive — and the rest falls into place.
Employee Onboarding SOP · the real checklist

Day One readiness first. Everything else follows.

Target: 100% Day One readiness, ≥85% new-hire satisfaction, ≥90% 90-day retention.
Grow & keep people

Once they're in, don't let them stall.

  1. Performance management — per the Performance Management SOP: SMART goals → ongoing coaching → mid-year review → self-assessment → annual evaluation → calibration → a 1–5 rating → development plan (PIP if it's a 1 or 2). Every rating needs documented evidence — never assign one from memory.
  2. Learning & development — per the Learning & Development SOP: start with a Training Needs Analysis, not a course catalogue. Build the skills the role and the company need next, not the ones that are easiest to deliver.
  3. Engagement — check the pulse before people quietly check out.
  4. Succession & talent — know who's next for every seat that matters, before it's empty.

Run the back office

The parts that must never be wrong.

  1. Compensation, benefits & payroll — per the Compensation & Benefits SOP: benchmark the market, check internal pay equity, then get approval before a single number changes. Confidentiality is rule one — never disclose an individual's pay.
  2. Attendance & leave — per the Attendance & Leave Management SOP: verify eligibility before you approve, reconcile attendance against payroll monthly, and keep every medical certificate confidential.
  3. Records management — employee data accurate and current, nothing stale.
  4. HR reporting & analytics — the KPI dashboard tells you the state of the team at a glance.
Handle the hard things

Early, documented, fair.

  1. Employee relations & grievance — every complaint logged, investigated, and closed — early, documented, private.
  2. Discipline & industrial relations — handled to one fair, consistent standard, never ad hoc.
  3. Compliance — per the HR Compliance SOP: monitor regulatory change before it lands, run the gap assessment, update the policy, then train and audit. Never implement an unapproved policy. MOM / IRAS / CPF reporting and work pass renewals are on time, every time.
  4. Offboarding — per the Offboarding & Employee Separation SOP: knowledge transfer and work handover happen before the exit interview; assets and access come back before final payroll clears.
Offboarding & Employee Separation SOP · the exit checklist

The order protects the business.

  1. Notify & review notice — every separation documented in writing, notice obligations confirmed.
  2. Knowledge transfer & work handover — critical responsibilities documented and a successor assigned before the exit interview.
  3. Exit interview — structured, confidential, kept neutral.
  4. Assets back, access off — equipment recovered and every system account disabled before clearance.
  5. Final payroll, then clearance — salary, leave balance and claims independently verified before sign-off.
The rule that never bends: no access removal = security risk. No knowledge transfer = business disruption.
Part two

Human vs cron

Know exactly which one owns which task.

The split · automate the mundane, own the people

Cron jobs

automate the mundane
  • Leave & attendance tracking
  • Onboarding checklists & reminders
  • Contract & record filing
  • Recurring HR reports
  • Payroll runs
  • Birthday / anniversary / probation reminders

You

own the people
  • Hiring calls
  • Performance & growth conversations
  • Grievances & discipline
  • Culture
  • Succession decisions
  • Anything sensitive
The test: if it repeats exactly the same way every time, it's a cron job. If it needs judgment or a relationship, it's yours.
Compliance & confidentiality · brief
  • Employee data is highly sensitive — treat it as need-to-know, always.
  • Follow the HR compliance SOP for MOM / IRAS / CPF reporting and work pass renewals — deadlines are real, not soft.
  • Keep records accurate and private — no shortcuts, no guessing.
  • Escalate anything legal or serious immediately — don't sit on it.
