Part two

The core moves

Cut, speed, motion — the muscle memory of a fast edit.

Building the rough cut · four moves

Cut, trim, split, ripple-delete.

MoveWhat it doesWhen
SplitCuts one clip into two at the playheadTo isolate a moment
TrimDrags a clip edge to shorten or extendTo top-and-tail
DeleteRemoves a clip, leaves a gap behindRarely — leaves holes
Ripple-deleteRemoves a clip and closes the gapYour default — no dead air
Ripple-delete is the workhorse. Split at the dead moment, ripple-delete it, and the timeline tightens itself. Cut after every phrase, keep the pace fast, kill every pause.
Speed · control the energy
Speed ramps move the emotion, not just the clip.

Slow a moment down to let it land, or ramp up to drive energy through a montage. Use CapCut's curve speed to ramp smoothly in and out rather than a hard jump — and always keep the story readable. Fast for pace, slow for feeling, never speed for its own sake.

Keyframes · motion done right

Two keyframes = one clean move.

  1. Set the start — put the playhead where the move begins, add a keyframe on position, scale or opacity.
  2. Set the end — move the playhead forward, change the value; CapCut fills the motion between.
  3. Ease it — smooth the in and out so the move feels premium, never robotic.
Keyframes drive every title and b-roll move — slide-ups, the money "ka-ching" reveal, subtle push-ins. On-brand motion supports the story and never distracts.
Transitions · less is more
A hard cut is a transition. Use it most of the time.

Reach for a transition only when it earns its place — a clean whoosh into a reveal, a match cut that carries the story. Skip the flashy library effects. Sound effects and motion carry the energy; the cut carries the story.

Part three

Text & captions

Auto-caption, correct, colour-code — then set the exact specs.

Captions · let CapCut do the first pass

Auto-caption, then correct — always.

  1. Run auto-captions — CapCut transcribes the whole track in seconds. This is your draft, never your final.
  2. Correct every line — fix typos, punctuation, names and mis-hears; auto-captions are wrong on brand names and numbers.
  3. Sync the timing — cut after every phrase so each line matches the voice, then colour-code by speaker.
Never ship raw auto-captions. The subtitle checklist: no typos, proper punctuation, easy to read, clear colours, timing synced, on-brand, inside the safe area.
The caption colour code · learn it cold

Colour tells the viewer who's speaking.

These are the subtitle colours you apply as content — not the deck's colours. Red Green Blue (RGB) hex values:

Part four

Audio

Raw voice, sound effects, music, ducking.

Sound · clean voice first, everything else supports it
Raw footage audioVolume set to max. Fade in and fade out both 0.0s. The guest's voice is the story — keep it clean and present.
Sound effectsUse the assets in the CapCut Space Library. Match each effect to the video's tone — serious, professional or entertaining — then balance and time it so it supports the cut without overpowering it.
MusicUnderneath the voice, not over it. Sits low as a bed to set energy and mood.
DuckingDrop the music when someone speaks. Lower the music track under the voice — automatically or with keyframes — so the words always cut through.
The rule: if you can't hear every word clearly, the mix is wrong. Voice on top, music ducked underneath, effects punctuating the cut.
