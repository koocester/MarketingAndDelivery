Part one

The client relationship

Onboard, deliver, keep them happy, grow it.

At signing · ownership transfer

Sales hands over. CX owns from the introduction onward.

Sales · before and at signing

  • Documents the signed scope, exclusions and promises
  • Creates the client group and adds CX
  • Introduces CX as the single contact
  • Answers for commercial accuracy

CX · from the introduction

  • Accepts the complete handoff
  • Owns client communication and onboarding
  • Schedules the internal pre-brief
  • Leads the client kickoff and ongoing cadence
The handoff is not complete until the brief is documented, CX is introduced in the client group and the internal pre-brief is booked.

CX owns the communication

Onboard the client and set them up to win.

  1. Join through Sales' introduction — the client knows CX is now the single contact
  2. Confirm scope, exclusions and success — remove ambiguity before work starts
  3. Gather assets and access — everything the internal team needs, upfront
  4. Set the communication cadence — channel, response expectation and update rhythm
  5. Send the kickoff recap — decisions, owners and next dates in writing

Part two

Keep them for life

The retention and recurring-revenue playbook, drawn from the people who wrote the books.

The mindset shift
Farm the client. Don't hunt the next one.

Hunting

one-off, always chasing new
  • Win the deal, then move on
  • Value ends at delivery
  • Revenue resets to zero every month

Farming

recurring, growing what you have
  • Win the deal, then nurture it
  • Value compounds after delivery
  • Revenue recurs and expands

Guy Nirpaz, Farm Don't Hunt. A client you keep and grow is worth far more than one you chase. At Koocester that means recurring engagements, not single projects.

The first 100 days decide it

Every new client walks eight emotional phases.

1 · Assess
weighing us up
2 · Admit
they sign, day one
3 · Affirm
buyer's remorse hits
4 · Activate
first real use
5 · Acclimate
learning the ropes
6 · Accomplish
first result lands
7 · Adopt
it becomes a habit
8 · Advocate
they refer others
Coleman's First 100 Days: up to 70% of new clients leave early because they feel neglected. Phase 3, Affirm, is buyer's remorse in week one. Get there first with a proactive check-in and an early win, and you guide them all the way to Advocate.
See churn coming

Score every client's health. Act before they slip.

Healthyengaged, seeing results, on track to renew
Watchquiet, slipping engagement, check in now
At riskdisengaged, no results felt, save now
Score on four signals: Deployment, Engagement, Adoption, and Return on Investment (Gainsight's health-score model). A health score makes you proactive, not reactive: you reach a slipping client before they reach for the exit.
Catch the drift

Learn the churn signals. Run the save.

Warning signs

  • Gone quiet, replies slow down
  • Engagement or usage drops off
  • Skipped the last review
  • A new decision-maker appears
  • Asking about price, not results

The save play

  • Reach out fast, in person
  • Revisit the goal they hired us for
  • Show the progress made so far
  • Agree one clear next win
  • Re-book the next review before you leave

Nirpaz, farming discipline. Maximise retention, minimise churn: a save costs a fraction of a new client.

A Quarterly Business Review is a scheduled sit-down every three months. For Koocester: show the leads delivered and the cost per lead, connect them to the client's revenue goal, and agree the next quarter. Gainsight makes this cadence the backbone of retention.
Easy beats impressive
Loyalty comes from ease, not from wow.
96%
of high-effort clients turn disloyal
9%
of low-effort clients turn disloyal
Matthew Dixon, The Effortless Experience. Across 97,000 customers, exceeding expectations barely moved loyalty, but every extra step, chase, or confusing reply cost the renewal. Measure it with a Customer Effort Score: "We made it easy." Remove friction before you add delight.
