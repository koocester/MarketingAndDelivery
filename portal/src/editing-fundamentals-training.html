Not the brand look — the craft of the cut. How you hook, hold and move a viewer through time so they feel something and never swipe.

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you hit it — and the final gate checks you against it.

The real job
Editing is controlling attention through time.

You are not trimming clips. You decide what the viewer sees, when they see it, and how they feel about it — second by second. Every cut is a choice about their attention and their emotion. That is the whole craft.

Part one

The hook

A viewer decides in three seconds. So never open with a slow intro, a logo, or "hi guys". Start on the best moment you have — the punchline, the reveal, the reaction — then earn the rest.

The test: if the first frame doesn't stop a thumb, nothing after it matters. Front-load your strongest second.
Part two

Pacing

Cut with the energy. Fast where it's hot, slow where it's deep.

Pacing · match the cut to the energy

Fast when it's hot. Breathe when it's deep.

High energy · fast cuts, tightEmotional beat · let it breathe

Speed up through high-energy moments — quick cuts drive momentum. Then slow down and hold on the emotional beats: a pause, a look, a line landing. Pacing is contrast. All-fast is exhausting; all-slow is dead.

When to cut · every cut earns its place

Cut here →

  • On the action — cut on a movement, it hides the join
  • The moment a beat is finished — don't linger
  • When a new cut adds information or energy
  • To jump the viewer forward, past the boring bit

Cut it out ✕

  • Dead air — silence, "umms", the wind-up before the point
  • Repeats — the same idea said twice
  • Anything that doesn't move the story or the feeling
  • A cut just to cut — every cut must earn its place
The rule: if a second isn't earning its place, it's costing you the viewer. When in doubt, cut it.
Rhythm & music · cut to the track
The track sets the cadence.

Cut on the beat. Line your edits up to the rhythm of the music — the drop, the downbeat, the change — and the video feels intentional instead of random. Use sound to drive energy: a rising track lifts a montage, a beat under a reveal makes it hit harder.

Feel the beats per minute (BPM). A fast track wants fast cuts; a slow one wants space. Fighting the music is the fastest way to make an edit feel off.
Part three

Holding them

Retention, variety, and the feeling that makes it land.

Retention · tighten relentlessly
Remove anything that makes them swipe.

Every video has swipe points — the moment attention dips and a thumb moves. Your job is to find and kill them: the slow bit, the repeat, the pause with nothing in it. Watch your own edit as a stranger would and cut ruthlessly until nothing sags.

Tighten twice. Your first cut is always too long. Pass through again and pull out another 10% — it almost always plays better.
B-roll & variety · show, don't tell

Cover the words with the thing.

Cover cutsB-roll hides joins and jumps. Lay supporting footage over an edit and a hard cut disappears — you can tighten aggressively without the seam showing.
Show, don't tellWhen they say it, show it. Someone mentions the car, the dish, the view — cut to it. Visuals land faster and deeper than words alone.
Variety holds attentionChange the shot. A new angle, a wide, a detail, a graphic — variety keeps the eye fresh. Same frame too long is a swipe point.
Emotion · the edit should make people feel
A clean cut is nothing if it doesn't feel.

This is the Koocester difference. The edit is not there to be tidy — it's there to make someone feel something: awe, curiosity, warmth, the ka-ching of a win. Hold the reaction shot a beat longer. Let the music swell. Build the moment, then land it.

Ties to storytelling. Pacing, music and the held beat are your tools for emotion. The story is the what; your cut is the how it feels.
The ending · land the call to action

Carry the energy into the ask.

Don't let the video die before the call to action (CTA). Hold the momentum right to the end, then land the ask cleanly — the comment keyword, the follow, the offer — while the viewer is still feeling it. A strong edit that fumbles the last three seconds leaks the lead you just earned.

End on a high, not a fade. The CTA lands best on the emotional peak, not after the energy has already drained away.
The difference that matters

Technically correct is not the same as holding.

 Technically correctHolds attention
OpeningStarts at the beginning, tidy introOpens on the strongest moment
PacingEven, steady, "correct"Fast where hot, breathes where deep
CutsClean joins, all on timeEvery cut earns its place, dead air gone
FeelNothing wrong with itMakes the viewer feel something
Anyone can make a correct edit. Your job is the one that holds — hooks in 3 seconds, moves with rhythm, and lands with emotion.
