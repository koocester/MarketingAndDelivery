Reading yourfinance dashboard

Your dashboard tells you which cash lever to pull next — if you know how to read it. This is the method. All figures in Singapore Dollars (S$).

Your mission

Read the money, and act before it becomes a problem.

Your dashboard shows the state of the company's cash: what we hold, who owes us, what we're spending, and what we've earned. Your job is to read it on a rhythm and turn each tile into one action — while there's still time to change the outcome.

What success looks like
You're winning when…
You read it weeklyon a rhythm, not once a quarter when someone asks
You know what each tile is telling youcash, receivables, spend, margin, invoices — at a glance
You act on the lead indicatorschase, send, flag — before they turn into a cash problem
You never present a number you can't source"not yet measurable" beats a confident guess, every time

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you get there — and the final gate checks you against it.

Part one

Where the numbers come from

Trust the source. The dashboard is only a mirror.

The always-on layer feeds the tiles

You don't type the numbers. The pipeline pulls them.

Finance tools → n8n · the always-on layer → The database → Your dashboard

n8n runs 24/7 on its own connection. It pulls from the finance tools — the accounting ledger, the bank and card feeds — and pipes those numbers into the database, which the dashboard reads. Nobody keys the tiles by hand.

The most important idea in this module
The dashboard is a mirror, not the source.

A tile only reflects what the pipeline last put in the database. So trust the source — the accounting ledger is the truth, the tile is a reflection of it. If the reflection looks wrong, suspect the mirror before you believe the number.

A stale tile is a broken sync, not a real number. If a tile hasn't updated in days, the pipeline stopped feeding it — the figure is old, not fact. Fix the sync; don't act on the frozen number.
Part two

Lead versus lag

One kind you can still act on. One kind is already history.

The key idea

Lag is what happened. Lead is what predicts it.

Lead indicators

act on these — the lever is still in your hand
  • Receivables ageing — how overdue we're getting
  • Spend rate versus budget
  • Invoices sitting unsent

Lag indicators

the result, already booked
  • Revenue booked
  • Cash in the bank
Act on the lead ones. By the time cash in the bank drops, the cause happened weeks ago. The lead tiles — ageing, spend rate, unsent invoices — are where you can still change the outcome.

Your finance tiles · read each as a trigger

Cash position — what's in the bank now→Ask: is our runway healthy? Is there enough to cover what's committed?
Receivables + ageing — who owes us, and how overdue→Chase the overdue ones first, oldest and largest at the top.
Spend vs budget — where we're over or under→Flag the overspend to the owner before month-end, not after.
Gross margin trend — what we keep on each dollar→Protect it — if the line is sliding, find which cost or price moved.
Invoices outstanding — work done, not yet billed→Send them — an unsent invoice is cash you're choosing not to collect.

Your OMTM · the One Metric That Matters this month
Pick one lead number, and move it: overdue receivables.

Each month, name the single number that most predicts cash health and watch it daily. Overdue receivables is a strong default — it's money you've already earned, and chasing it is the fastest, cheapest cash you'll ever raise. Drive it toward zero and cash follows.

One number, watched. Cash in the bank is the score; overdue receivables is the lever. Pick the lever as your One Metric That Matters, and the score takes care of itself.
The honesty rule · non-negotiable
A number you can't source is a number you don't trust.

Some figures look important but have no real source wired behind them yet. Burn rate and runway are the classic trap — if no true burn source is connected, the runway tile is a guess dressed as a fact.

Say "not yet measurable" — never fabricate a runway. If a burn or runway figure has no real source behind it, report that plainly. A confident wrong number is worse than an honest gap, because people act on it.

Build the habit

Read on a rhythm. Not once a quarter.

Weekly

10 minutes
  • Chase every overdue receivable
  • Send every unsent invoice
  • Flag any spend running over budget

Monthly

30 minutes
  • Gross margin trend — up, flat, or sliding?
  • Re-set your OMTM for the month
  • Confirm every source is still syncing
A quarter is too late. Overdue receivables and overspend compound quietly — caught weekly they're a chase; caught quarterly they're a hole.
