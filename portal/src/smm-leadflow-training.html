The standard · non-negotiable
A post with no working flow is a leak.

Views and comments mean nothing if the DM never fires, the magnet never delivers, or the lead never gets captured. Test the flow end to end before the post ships — not after someone complains they never heard back.

Part two

Sequencing

One DM is not a flow. Build the sequence.

Build it like an email sequence

Don't stop at one DM.

  1. Instant value — deliver exactly what they asked for, immediately.
  2. Follow-up — more value or proof, a beat later.
  3. The offer — the soft CTA, once trust is built.
  4. Re-engage — one nudge to whoever went quiet.
  5. Hand-off or nurture — warm leads go to Sales, cold ones stay in the drip.
Same logic as an email drip
Space the messages. Match the message to where they are.

A lead who just opted in isn't ready for the offer, and a lead who's gone cold doesn't need more value — they need one clean re-engage. Sequencing is reading the moment, not blasting the same message on a timer.

Part three

Posting & scheduling

Cadence and consistency.

The cadence · non-negotiable basics
  • A content calendar — know what's posting and when, before the day arrives.
  • Post at the page's best times — when the audience is actually there.
  • Platform-native formatting — never a straight re-post across platforms.
  • Caption + hook + CTA — every post earns its own scroll-stop and its own ask.
Consistency beats sporadic bursts. A steady cadence compounds; a flurry of posts followed by silence resets you every time.
Audience-active time − lead-time = post time
  1. Check the platform's analytics for when YOUR audience is online — not a generic "best time to post" list.
  2. Post 30–60 minutes before that peak — gives the algorithm lead-time to pick it up before your audience arrives.
  3. Test 2–3 windows over a few weeks; compare saves and reach, not just likes.
  4. Lock the winner. Re-test only if performance drifts.
Every post · no exceptions
Capture the post URL. Mark the actual post date.

The moment it's live, log the URL and the real date it posted — not the date it was scheduled for. This is what makes performance trackable and what every dashboard downstream depends on.

Part four

Handling comments

Triage fast. Every comment is reach.

Triage fast · a comment a day doesn't wait

Engage →

  • Reply to real questions
  • Pin the best comment
  • Reply, converse — community is the moat
  • Route buying-intent comments to DM

Handle ✕

  • Hide or handle spam and trolls
  • Don't let noise bury real questions
  • Don't leave buying-intent sitting in public
Reply. Converse. Community is the moat.
Trolls happen · protect the room

Hate comments aren't a content problem. They're a protection duty.

Do →

  • Don't feed it — no public back-and-forth with a troll
  • Respond once with grace, or hide/report abuse and move on
  • Keep genuine criticism up — disagreement isn't hate
  • If it targets the guest, step in fast and shield them

Don't ✕

  • Don't argue in public — it feeds the algorithm and the troll
  • Don't reflexively delete every negative comment
  • Don't leave a guest exposed and unanswered
  • Don't mirror the tone — grace de-escalates, matching energy doesn't
Guests are a special case. We invited them on — their reputation is on us. If hate targets a guest or interviewee, shield them fast: hide or report abusive comments, respond with grace, and don't let it sit.
Why this matters
Every comment is reach and a relationship.

A reply keeps someone in the algorithm's eye and in yours. Ignored comments cost you both — the engagement, and the person who might have become a lead.
