The mindset
AI is a force-multiplier, not a replacement.

It does not replace you. It multiplies you. The Large Language Model (LLM) is the sharpest junior you've ever had — tireless, fast, and only as good as the direction you give it. You stay the one in charge.

Part one

When to reach for it

And when to keep your hands on the wheel.

Where AI earns its keep · and where it doesn't

Reach for it →

  • Ideation — angles, hooks, names, first ideas
  • First drafts — captions, emails, briefs, outlines
  • Research & summarising — digest long things fast
  • Restructuring — reshape messy notes into order
  • Coding & automation — scripts, workflows, formulas
  • Analysis — spot patterns, sanity-check numbers

Keep it human ✕

  • The final judgment call — the decision is yours to own
  • Costly-if-wrong, unchecked — anything a mistake would hurt, without a human review
  • True secrets — passwords, logins, payment details, personal identifiers (client strategy & campaign work goes into Poppy)

AI does the heavy lifting. The call, the check, and the confidential stuff stay with you.

Part two

Guardrails

Non-negotiable. These three protect us and our clients.

Guardrail 1 · confidentiality
Never paste confidential data into a public AI tool.

A client's private brief, internal financials, personal data — none of it goes into a public chatbot. Assume anything you paste could be stored or seen. If it's confidential, keep it out. No exceptions.

The test: could exposing this cause significant financial or reputational harm to Koocester or a client? If yes, or the answer is on the edge, keep it out of public AI tools.
Guardrail 2 · you own the output
AI can be confidently wrong. Always verify.

A Large Language Model (LLM) will invent facts, figures and sources and state them with total confidence — this is called a hallucination. Never ship what it gives you unchecked. You put your name on the output, so the result is yours to verify — every claim, every number, every quote.

Source rule: always ask for relevant citations. Open every link and confirm it leads to the stated website or article and actually supports the claim. A citation-looking link is not proof.
Guardrail 3 · honesty
Disclose AI use where honesty requires it.

AI is a tool, and using it is not something to hide. But where being open matters — a client expecting your own work, a claim of authorship, anything where the truth would change how someone reads it — say so. Never let AI make us look dishonest.

Part three

Getting good outputs

Garbage in, garbage out. Great brief in, great work out.

Brief it like you'd brief a sharp junior

A prompt opens the work. A loop gets it to done.

Briefcontext + done
Draftfirst pass
Critiquefind the gap
Improvefix the gap
Verify ↺pass or loop
  1. Start with evidence — give the source files, examples, constraints and audience
  2. Name the test — define what must be true, checked or approved at the end
  3. Run the critique — ask what is weak, missing, inconsistent or unverified
  4. Loop deliberately — feed the evidence back in until the test passes or approval is needed
Every pass reports three things: evidence, remaining gap and next action.
Go past a single prompt

Chain the steps. Feed the output back in.

The power isn't in one clever prompt — it's in the loop. Take one output and make it the next input. When a loop is worth running again and again, that's where automation comes in: build it once, let it run.

Your job now
Move from doing every step to directing the system.

Your value shifts upward: from typing every word to defining the outcome, supplying context, reviewing quality and making the call. When a session works repeatedly, turn it into a playbook. When the playbook stabilises, turn it into a skill, agent or automation.

The standard: each month, identify one repeated workflow to improve, document or automate without weakening ownership or review.
