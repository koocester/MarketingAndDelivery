The KoocesterSales Process

The seven-step selling process. This is how we take a stranger and turn them into a signed, happy, long-term Koocester client.

The mindset
Selling is a process, not a pitch.

Great selling is not luck and it is not one clever line. It is seven repeatable steps, run in order, every time. Master the process and closing stops being a gamble and becomes a habit.

What good selling looks like
You're selling the Koocester way when…
You only chase quality prospectsbrands who can afford us, benefit from us, and value the spend
You arrive as a consultantyou know their business before the call, so you build a vision, not a quote
You sell outcomes, not videosgrowth, reach, trust and leads, framed as a partnership
You ask for the sale and follow upyou close with confidence, then nurture the relationship for the long term

This is the bar. The seven steps that follow are how you hit it, and the final gate checks you against it.

Step 1 · what a quality client looks like · tap each one

Three signs you have a good one.

1
Does not fixate only on price
They ask what they get, not just what it costs. Price is a factor, never the only conversation.

Step two

2

Be ready

Know the company before any call, so you answer with confidence.

Step 2 · preparation

Show up as a consultant, not a vendor.

Before any call or meeting, do the homework so you can build a vision for them:

  1. Study their business model how they actually make money
  2. Review their social media and ads what they run today
  3. Identify content gaps where they are missing
  4. Understand their target audience who they need to reach
  5. Decide which platform fits Main, Homes, Foodie, and so on
Preparation is what separates a consultant from a salesperson. Walk in already knowing their business and you own the room.
Step three

3

Approach

Your first personal connection. Get them interacting.

Step 3 · the approach

Build rapport before you build a pitch.

This is your first personal connection. Personalise it, get the prospect interacting, and ask questions that make them talk about their business. People buy from people they trust, so earn the conversation first.

Ask questions like
"What are you looking for in working with Koocester?"
And
"What do you feel is currently lacking in the marketing side of your business?"
Step four

4

Presentation

Frame Koocester as the solution to their problem.

Step 4 · the presentation
You do not present videos.

You present

the real product
  • Business outcomes
  • Content strategy
  • Distribution reach
  • Asset value

You explain

why it works for them
  • Why video works for their business
  • How Koocester creates and distributes
  • Which platform fits best
  • How it drives trust, leads or sales
The goal: make Koocester feel like a growth partner, not a video vendor. Tailor everything to the problem you uncovered.
Step five

5

Handling objections

An objection is uncertainty, not rejection. It is an opportunity.

Step 5 · handling objections

Doubt is a door, not a wall.

Common objections

what you will hear
  • Price
  • Return on investment
  • Audience relevance
  • Internal approvals

How to handle them

reduce risk, add clarity
  • Compare value versus traditional ads
  • Emphasise asset reusability
  • Highlight targeted sub-accounts
  • Show past brand collaborations
The goal: reduce the perceived risk and increase clarity. Every objection you answer well moves them closer to yes.
Step 5 · objection scripts

Answers you can reach for.

"It is too expensive."
"What you pay for is the branding and exposure of your business to the public. Given our following and track record, the price is worth it."
"There is no guaranteed return."
"Marketing is never a 100 percent guarantee, but to support you we run boosting at no extra charge to guarantee 300,000 views across Instagram, Facebook and TikTok."
"Let me think about it."
"Sure. Is there a concern I can help clear up right now?"
Step six

6

Closing

Once they are convinced, ask for the sale. Make the decision easy.

Step 6 · the close
Do not ask "Would you like to proceed?"

Once they are convinced, guide the decision instead of asking for permission. Confirm their goals, recommend the best package, and set the next step. Make sure they understand the terms, and upsell where it fits.

Guide, do not ask
"The next step is to lock a shoot date and confirm whether we post on the main page or a sub-division page."
The goal: make the decision easy and logical. You are steering to a clear next action, not waiting for a maybe.
Step 6 · closing techniques

Three ways to guide the yes.

Assume close
"Which package would you like to proceed with, the 8,000 dollar trial?"
Fear-of-loss close
"Your competitor already signed a 3-video package, so the leads would go to them. You would want a fair share too, right?"
Alternative close
"Would you go for the 6,000 dollar trial or the 8,000 dollar trial?"
The rule: never tell a client "you can think about it and come back to me." Guide the decision now.
Step seven

7

Follow up

After the sale, nurture the relationship. Do not keep selling.

Step 7 · after the close

Turn one-time clients into long-term partners.

Right after the sale

get them live smoothly
  • Reconfirm deliverables and timelines
  • Ensure a smooth onboarding
  • Over-deliver on communication

After the campaign

grow the relationship
  • Share performance feedback
  • Suggest next content angles
  • Upsell a retainer or next campaign
  • Ask for referrals or testimonials
The goal: nurture, do not pitch. A well-served client becomes your best source of the next sale.
The engine behind step one

Lead generation and prospecting

Sales is a numbers game. Fill the top of the funnel every day.

Where leads come from

Two types. Four main sources.

Lead types

know which you have
  • Warm leads
  • Cold leads

Sources

where to find them
  • Social media accounts: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok
  • Previous connections or customers from past companies
  • Referrals
  • WhatsApp broadcast group
Match the script to the relationship. New leads get a short introduction: who you are, your company, your role, and why you are reaching out. Known contacts get a reconnect: who you are, where you worked together, your company and role now, and why you are reconnecting.
Sales is a numbers game

Volume creates conversion.

15 to 20
cold leads reached per day, per salesperson
75 to 100
prospects reached per week
5 to 7
calls or meetings set per day
  • Keep the message clear and punchy about the value you build
  • Follow up frequently, do not let leads go cold
  • Keep a clear pipeline to track every lead and follow-up
The find-and-message method

Find the businesses. Send the message.

Find them

build the list
  • Search Koocester niches on TikTok
  • Scrape followers of Koocester and sub accounts
  • Collect leads from sponsored ads

Message them

15 to 20 per person, per day, from a Koocester admin phone
  • Tell them who we are
  • Mention their brand or industry
  • Say what you offer and name-drop someone relevant
  • Request a call or meeting with a deadline
Let AI do the heavy lifting. Use it to generate hyper-personalised outbound tailored to each industry, so every message lands like it was written just for them.
The other half of step seven

After sales and upselling

The relationship after the sale is where recurring revenue lives.

After sales and upselling

Stay warm. Watch the numbers. Upsell on time.

After sales

keep it human
  • Check in 1 to 2 weeks after the video launches with a simple report update
  • Keep the conversation warm
  • Maintain a genuine relationship

Upselling

time it right
  • Know the package you sold and track how many videos have posted, so you know when to upsell the recurring deal
  • Find out how the client's video is performing, so you know how to approach the recurring upsell
The mindset that carries it all
More activity in. More closes out.
  • The more prospects you approach, the more meetings and calls you generate
  • The more meetings and calls, the higher the conversion
  • Be confident in your product
  • Enjoy the journey and the process
  • Be genuine and real
