Part two · gifts & barter

The Gifts & Barter rules

The everyday policy that lands in your inbox most. In two parts — what to declare, and how we operate. Know them cold.

Why this matters

Gifts and barter are constant. You are personally accountable for how they're handled.

As a media company covering homes, food, autos and wealth, we're offered free products, meals, trips and barter deals constantly. Handled well, that's normal business. Handled wrong, it's a reputational risk (readers stop trusting our coverage), an editorial-integrity risk (coverage starts looking bought), and a tax risk (undeclared value is still value). Declare everything — that one habit is what protects the brand, and it's on every one of us, every time, no exceptions.

The Gifts & Barter rules · declare & thresholds
  1. Declare everything. Any gift, meal, trip, product, or barter offer through your Koocester work goes in the Register — even ones you decline.
  2. Token (≤ S$50) — e.g. a meal or sample. Log only, no approval needed.
  3. Over S$50, or tied to coverage — log it and get manager approval before you accept.
  4. High value, or could look like influence — decline and escalate. No exceptions.
The Gifts & Barter rules · how we operate
  1. Never solicit. Never ask for gifts or discounts using Koocester's name.
  2. Coverage is never for sale. A gift never buys or changes what, whom, or how we feature or edit.
  3. Barter is a documented deal, not a handshake. Defined deliverables, approved by the Head of Growth and Finance, recorded like any client.
  4. Disclose to the audience where platforms require it — gifted or paid-partnership labels, used honestly.
  5. Know who owns it. High-value gifts are company property. Staff keep only token items.

When in doubt — declare and ask. Asking first is always the right move. It is never a mistake.
