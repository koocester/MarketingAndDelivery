Koocester Academy · Producer

How toshoot

Shoot day, start to finish. Clean footage for every HEART row, backed up before you leave. 1.5 to 4 hours on location.

How to win

Every HEART row has footage, audio is clean, everything is backed up.

Shoot day is done when every row in the brief has A-roll and B-roll, the audio is clean, and it is all uploaded, before you leave the location. Not after. Before.

What success looks like
You're winning when…
You're on location 30 minutes earlythe guest never arrives before you do
The hook shot is captured firstthe most important shot in the video, not an afterthought
Audio plays back cleanno hum, no traffic, no wind — checked on headphones
Footage is backed up by HEART folderbefore you leave the location, not after

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you hit it — and the final gate checks you against it.

Why it matters
Three things kill shoots. None can be fixed in editing.
  • Bad audio — you cannot clean voice that was recorded dirty.
  • Missing supporting shots — no B-roll means the editor has nothing to cut to.
  • Wrong framing — a badly framed shot is a lost shot.

This SOP makes all three rare.

Three rules that never bend
Reach early
30 minutes minimum before the guest. This is non-negotiable, the 30 minutes decides the whole day.
The start is everything
The hook visual is the most important shot in the video. If the first frame does not stop the scroll, nothing else matters.
Producer energy
The guest matches your tone. Lazy producer, lazy answers. High energy producer, high energy video.
Two terms, one order

A-roll = the interview footage, what the guest says. B-roll = every supporting shot, close-ups, wides, action.

B-roll first→ A-roll→ Final B-roll fill
Why B-roll first? You scout the location while shooting, the guest gets comfortable seeing the camera before they are on it, and rapport builds so the interview happens with a relaxed guest. Relaxed guest, better answers, better video.

Part two

On site · the first 30 minutes

Reach early. Set up before the guest.

Arrival & site assessment
  • A-roll spot — quiet (no traffic, aircon hum, fridge buzz), background contrasts with the guest's wardrobe, soft natural light (window light is best).
  • B-roll spots — list them by the HEART row they serve. Hook visual first, it is shot #1.
  • Test audio in the A-roll spot on headphones, listen for traffic, aircon, fridge, echo, wind. Bad noise, move the spot.
  • Lock exposure & white balance — tap and hold on the guest's face for AE/AF lock, slide to adjust brightness.
Guest arrival · five minutes
  1. Welcome warmly, offer water.
  2. Clip the mic on, inside the collar, pointing toward the mouth.
  3. Recap the rules in 60 seconds: faceless format so the voice carries everything, answer in complete sentences starting with the topic not "yeah", 30–60 seconds per answer, pause and restart anytime.
  4. Walk the guest to the A-roll spot, show the framing.
  5. Mic test, guest counts "one, two, three, four, five", listen on headphones.
Part three

Capture

B-roll, then A-roll.

B-roll capture · hook visual first

Always

  • Roll 1–2 seconds BEFORE the action
  • Each clip 3 seconds minimum, 5 is better
  • Subject moving OR camera moving, never both static
  • Multiple angles of every important thing
  • Wipe the lens, move with your legs for stability

Never

  • Static camera + static subject (unusable)
  • Clips under 3 seconds (editor can't use them)
  • Trust the screen, tap to check focus
Coverage rule: every HEART row needs at least one B-roll clip, roughly one clip per 5 seconds of scene. 2 strong clips beat 5 weak ones.
Composition · easy to remember
  • Rule of thirds — place the subject on a grid line, not always centred.
  • Don't cut at joints — the frame edge should not end at neck, wrist, knee or ankle. Cut mid-chest or hip.
  • Layer depth — something close, the subject in the middle, something blurry behind. Three layers looks rich.
  • Lead room — leave space on the side the subject is looking toward.
The 3-second rule: every clip at least 3 seconds. Editors trim down, they cannot make a clip longer. Storage is cheap, regret is expensive.
A-roll capture · the interview

Always

  • Roll BEFORE you ask the question
  • Say the take first: "Question 1, take 1"
  • 2–3 takes per question, even if the first sounds great
  • Record in 10-minute intervals
  • Listen back after EVERY question, no exceptions

Never

  • Say "uh-huh" or "right" while the guest talks (kills the audio)
  • Interrupt to correct, let them finish then redo
  • Trust the meter, listen on headphones
Coaching the guest · voice carries the video

If an answer is monotone or rushed, redo it.

  • "That was good, let's do one more with more energy."
  • "Slow down on the punchline, let the number land."
  • "Smile when you say that, it comes through in the voice."
  • "Talk like you're explaining to a friend, not pitching to a crowd."
  • "Pause for a second before the surprising number, it gives it weight."
Movement: mix smooth and raw. Slow + smooth for calm sections, raw + handheld for the high-energy parts.
Question design SOP · yours to own

Five rules for questions that cut clean.

  1. One ask per question — combining two asks gets a tangled answer.
  2. Maximum ten words — cut filler like "walk me through the…"
  3. Specific over abstract — "This Ferrari" beats "your car."
  4. Force topic-first answers — phrase it so the guest starts with the topic, not a connective. This is the highest-leverage editing win.
  5. Short, confident, concise — say it once, cleanly.
This is yours. Producers ask the questions on the shoot, own the design, not just the delivery.
Coach the guest's tone · before each question

The faceless format means the guest's voice carries everything.

  • Vary the pace — slow on the punchline, fast on the setup.
  • Land the number — pause before and after key figures.
  • Vocal smile — smiles register even without a face on camera.
  • Avoid monotone — if it sounds flat, redo with more energy.
  • Conversational, not presentational — talk like explaining to a friend.
Frame it this way: you're directing a performance, not just recording answers.
The producer's mindset · on the shoot

Genuine reactions. Improv-ready follow-ups.

  • Genuine enthusiasm, only in the right moments — a real "wow" lands only when earned.
  • Improv-ready with burning follow-ups — when the guest drops a valuable answer, go off-script.
  • The right follow-up is the question the viewer would shout at the screen, or a thought-provoking, out-of-the-box question that makes both guest and viewer think.
Part four

Wrap & handoff

Fill the gaps, back up, notify.

Final B-roll fill & wrap
Every HEART row has B-roll? If not, capture it now
Did the guest mention something we have no visual for? Shoot it
Final shot-list check, walk the brief row by row
Audio playback, one take on headphones, clean voice no hum
Pack the kit, count items (never lose the mic)
Thank the guest, confirm the rough-cut delivery date, photograph any logos
Back up before you leave · upload same day
The editor cannot start without files in the right folders.

Back up on location (iCloud sync on, or manual to Google Drive). Drop every clip into the HEART row it was shot for:

[Shoot date]_[Guest name]/ ├── A-roll/ → H · E · A1 · A2 · A3 · R · T ├── B-roll/ → H · E · A1 · A2 · A3 · R · T └── Reference/ (brief, HEART outline, questions)

If a take serves two HEART rows, copy it into both. Update the Tracker, notify the editor.
