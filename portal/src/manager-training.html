TheManager

You build the people and systems that keep the pipeline flowing.

Your mission

Clear the road, don't drive the car.

Your team owns the stages. You own the flow: spot where work is stuck, unblock it, and hold the standard. If nothing's overdue and nothing's stuck, you're winning.

What success looks like
You're winning when…
No bottleneck lives longyou spot the wall and clear it same day
Every role is on its lead timeno stage quietly running over its clock
You diagnose before you blamefind the system failure, then coach the accountable owner
The floor keeps movingwork flows end to end, nothing stalls silently

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you hold it — and the final gate checks you against it.

Where you sit

Above the pipeline, not inside it.

Sourcing→ Planning→ Shoot→ Editing→ QC→ Approval→ Upload

You watch the whole line. Your job starts where a record stops moving.

The manager switch

Employee mindset → owner mindset

A promotion is not only more responsibility. It changes how you think: from completing your own work to owning the conditions that let the whole team succeed.

Owner mindset · time

Measure output, not hours.

Employee mindset

“How many hours did I work?”

Owner mindset

“What outcome did those hours produce?”

The manager move: define the output, standard and deadline before work starts.

Reference: @itsalvinhuang · supplied by Hakim

Owner mindset · problems

Own the outcome. Assign the action.

Employee mindset

“That’s not my department.”

Owner mindset

Everything affecting the outcome deserves my attention—even when someone else owns the work.

The manager move: take responsibility for the result without taking every task back into your own hands.

Reference: @itsalvinhuang · supplied by Hakim

Owner mindset · feedback

Turn criticism into information.

Employee mindset

Takes criticism of the business personally.

Owner mindset

Treats criticism as information that can improve the business.

The manager move: separate ego from evidence, then convert feedback into a decision or test.

Reference: @itsalvinhuang · supplied by Hakim

Owner mindset · growth

Build capability beyond yourself.

Employee mindset

Get better at doing the work.

Owner mindset

Build people and systems that can do the work repeatedly.

The manager move: teach, document, delegate and inspect.

Reference: @itsalvinhuang · supplied by Hakim

Owner mindset · security

Make yourself replaceable in operations.

Employee mindset

Holds information and control because being needed feels safe.

Owner mindset

Shares knowledge and builds operations that do not depend on one person.

The manager move: if the team cannot run without you, the system is not finished.

Part one

Reading the base

Where to look, and what "good" looks like.

Your views

Manager · Pipeline is home.

It shows the whole flow by stage. Group by stage to see the shape of the queue, by editor or producer to see load. Read it top to bottom every morning.

The daily read, in order
  • What's overdue? Anything past its clock is a dropped ball. Chase it.
  • What's stuck at Not Started? The backlog isn't planned. Push it to the coordinator.
  • Where's the wall? The stage with the most records piled up is today's bottleneck.
  • Who's overloaded? Rebalance before someone drowns.
Part two

The guardrails

The gates that protect quality. Know what each one is for.

What a guardrail is
No record jumps a stage without passing a gate.

Each gate is a button someone has to press. That's deliberate: it stops half-baked work moving forward. Your job is to make sure the right person presses it, on time.

Video gates
Approve to plan· Approve QC· Final approval· Approve to upload
  • Approve to plan: good means the idea and guest fit the page's objective.
  • Approve QC: good means the edit meets standard and every note is actioned.
  • Final approval: good means you would proudly show it to the client.
Carousel gates
Start copywriting· Approve carousel

Start copywriting fires when a copywriter picks it up, so the copywriting clock starts fairly. Approve carousel: good means copy is on brand, error free, and true to the source video.

When something is stuck
  • Overdue at a gate? Diagnose the approval system first: named owner, visible deadline and clear approval standard. Then speak to the accountable person.
  • Bouncing in amendments? The notes weren't specific. Get one clear pass.
  • Aging in Ready to Upload? The SMM has a backlog. Rebalance or reprioritise.
Part three

Views & permissions

Build your own lens. Leave the shared ones alone.

Two kinds of view

Shared view

  • Everyone sees it
  • Editing it changes it for the whole team
  • Locked and standardised

Do not touch.

Personal view

  • Only you see it
  • Filter, sort, group freely
  • Your safe sandbox

Build as many as you like.

How to monitor without breaking anything

Always work in a personal view.

Create your own view, filter it to what you're chasing today, and leave the locked shared views exactly as they are. That's how you watch the team without disturbing their screens.

The rule of thumb
If a change helps only you, it's a personal view.

If it should change what the whole team sees, that's a decision, not a click. Raise it, don't just edit the shared view.

Your morning
  1. Read the morning brief in Manager Updates. It opens with the bottleneck and the one move for today.
  2. Make that move first, before anything else.
  3. Clear your approval gates. They run on clocks.
  4. Scan your team's queue for anything stuck beyond its clock.
  5. If someone is named in the brief, talk to them today, not Friday.
