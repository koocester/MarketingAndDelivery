Vision& direction

The first job of a leader: decide where the company is going, why it matters, and make everyone believe it. This is the skill of setting a north star — with Koocester's own as the worked example.

What direction actually is

Everyone can run fast. Direction decides where.

Below you, people optimise their lane — a better video, a faster close, a cleaner dashboard. Your job sits one level up: set the destination the whole company is running toward, and keep every lane pointed at it. Speed without direction is just motion. Your edge is deciding the where, then making it impossible to miss.

What success looks like
You've set direction when…
Anyone can state where we're going in one linea new hire and a client both get it, same day
Decisions ladder to the vision, not to the moodyou can trace any yes or no back to the destination
The company runs to a few clear betsnot fifty priorities that cancel each other out
Short-term pressure bends, the vision doesn'ta hard quarter changes tactics, never the north star

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you set it — and the final gate checks you against it.

Get this straight first · a vision is not a slogan

A destination that ladders every decision.

A vision IS →

  • A concrete destination you can picture arriving at
  • A test you can run a decision against — does this move us toward it?
  • Stable for years, so people can build on it
  • Specific enough to rule things out, not just in

A vision is NOT ✕

  • A slogan that sounds good and decides nothing
  • A wish list of everything you'd love to do
  • A target that changes with every hard month
  • So broad it fits any action, so it guides none
The test of a real vision: it makes some choices obviously wrong. If it can't say no, it isn't giving direction.

Part two

Turning vision into direction

A destination is useless until it moves today's decisions.

Translate the vision · into a few company bets

One north star. A handful of bets. Every decision.

Vision 2035

→translates to
3–5 bets
the few things that get us there this year
→sets
Team priorities
what each function owns
→drives
Daily decisions

The discipline is subtraction. A vision that produces twenty priorities has produced none. Name the few bets that move the north star most — and say no to the rest, however good they look.
Communicate it · the one-line test

A new hire and a client should get it in one line.

To a new hire

so they know what they're building
  • "We're building the growth partner businesses trust — and by 2035, the most trusted growth ecosystem in the world."
  • They can now judge their own work against it

To a client

so they know who they're buying from
  • "We don't sell content — we deliver growth, and our culture is why it works."
  • They can now see why we're different
If it takes a paragraph, it isn't set yet. A vision only travels if it's short enough to repeat and clear enough to act on.
Protect it · when the short term pushes back

Bend the plan. Never the north star.

Hold the line →

  • Change tactics, timelines and tools freely
  • Say no to fast money that pulls off the destination
  • Return to fundamentals under pressure, don't add noise
  • Let the vision be the reason behind the hard call

Watch for drift ✕

  • A hard quarter quietly rewrites where we're going
  • "Just this once" deals that don't ladder up
  • New shiny initiatives stacked on to relieve pressure
  • The destination moves so no one can hold you to it
Pressure is the test. A vision held only when it's easy was never direction — it was decoration. The hard season is where you prove it's real.
Decide by it · the ladder test

Every decision should ladder up to the vision.

  1. Name the destination — which part of Vision 2035 does this serve: media, education, or data intelligence?
  2. Trace the ladder — does this move a company bet forward, or just feel productive?
  3. Run the values filter — does it hold GUIDE, especially trust?
  4. Then ask "and then what?" — check the second and third-order effects before you commit.
  5. If it doesn't ladder, it's a no — however good it looks in isolation.
This is what makes vision real: not a poster on the wall, but the test you run before every yes. Do it out loud enough times and the whole company learns to run it too.
