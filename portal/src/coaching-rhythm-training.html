Why a rhythm beats good intentions
Without a cadence, coaching gets skipped.

Everyone means to coach. Then the week fills, the fire starts, and the 1-on-1 is the first thing to fall off. A rhythm removes the choice. When it's a fixed slot every week, coaching becomes inevitable — not something you get to if there's time.

The whole idea: a ritual you never negotiate beats good intentions you always renegotiate.
The division of labour

The system feeds the data. You hold the relationship.

The system's job

the numbers, surfaced
  • Dashboards show who hit their target
  • Lead-time signals show who's slow or stuck
  • The agenda arrives ready — no digging

Your job

the human, coached
  • Name the gap with care, not blame
  • Ask the question that unlocks it
  • Hold them to the commitment they made

You never have to invent the agenda. The data hands it to you — you bring the judgement and the warmth.

The weekly holds the momentum. The monthly holds the standard. One is the heartbeat, the other is the checkpoint. You need both.
The core unit · every weekly 1-on-1
One win. One gap. One commitment.
1 · One win
Name it
Start with something real they did well this week. Specific, from their numbers. Momentum before pressure.
2 · One gap
Off their data
The one number or lead-time signal that slipped. Pulled from their dashboard — not a vibe, not everything at once.
3 · One commitment
With a deadline
A specific next action they commit to, and the date it's done by. Written down. You'll check it next week.

Tight and repeatable. The same three beats every week — that's what makes it a rhythm, not a meeting.

Coaching off data
Come with the number, not a vibe.

The dashboards and lead-time signals already show who's slow, who's stuck, and who's flying. That is your coaching agenda — it's handed to you before the 1-on-1 even starts.

Coach off data →

  • "Your lead time slipped to 4 days twice this week."
  • "You hit target on three of five — the two misses were both carousels."
  • Specific, checkable, hard to argue with

Coach off a vibe ✕

  • "I feel like you've been a bit off lately."
  • "Try to be more on top of things."
  • Vague, deniable, goes nowhere

The number makes the conversation fair. It's about the work, not the person.

The monthly review · once a month, every report

Performance against the bar. Then the check.

  1. Performance against the bar — did they hit the standard this month? Numbers, not impressions.
  2. The reward / consequence check — strong month gets recognised; a miss gets named and actioned. Both, every time.
  3. Set the month ahead — what "great" looks like next month, and the one thing to lift.
This ties to the Rewards & consequences module. The weekly builds the habit; the monthly is where the standard is actually enforced — no strong month unnoticed, no weak month unaddressed.
Part two

How you coach

Name the gap — then ask, don't tell.

The coaching model
Name the gap. Then ask, don't tell.

A simple four-step shape. You raise the gap, then you walk them through it with questions — so the answer is theirs.

1
Goal
"What does good look like here?"
2
Reality
"What's actually happening — what do the numbers say?"
3
Options
"What could you try? What else?"
4
Will
"What will you do, and by when?"

Goal → Reality → Options → Will. The person finds the answer, so the person owns it.

Why questions, not answers
Advice short-circuits ownership. Questions build it.

When you hand someone the answer, you own the outcome — and they wait for you next time. When you ask the question that lets them find it, they own the outcome, and they own the next one too. It's slower once, and faster forever.

The rule: give the answer and you become the bottleneck. Ask the question and you build a person who doesn't need you for it.
How you listen

Listen to receive, not to reply.

Listen to receive

  • You're trying to understand, not to answer
  • Silence is fine — let them fill it
  • You hear the real issue under the first one
  • They talk more than you do

Listen to reply

  • You're loading your response while they talk
  • You jump in with the fix
  • You solve the surface problem and miss the real one
  • You talk more than they do

Hard truths said early and with care. The care is what lets the truth land.

Following up · the real test
A commitment un-checked next week isn't a commitment.

The coaching doesn't happen in the 1-on-1 — it happens when you follow up. Open next week on last week's commitment: done or not? Checked every time, it becomes real. Never checked, and every commitment quietly becomes optional.

Track it. Write the commitment down in the moment, and it's the first line of the next 1-on-1. Following up is the difference between a nice chat and a coach who changes people.
