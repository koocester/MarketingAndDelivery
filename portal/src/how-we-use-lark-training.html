Part one

Your work lives in the Base

Your view, your records, and the cells you cannot leave empty.

Your base, your view

One big Base. Your own private lens on it.

All of production sits in one Lark Base. You do not scroll the whole thing. Each role has a saved view that filters it down to only your work. Open your view and everything in front of you is yours to move.

Start here every time. Your view is your to-do list. If it is empty, you are clear. If it is full, you know exactly what is next.

Part two

Notifications and messaging

Act on the cards. Keep the noise in the right place.

When Lark pings you

The button click moves the work forward.

Producer Updates
Reel ready for your approval
Client hook reel, cut 2 is waiting on you. Approve to release it, or send it back with a note.

Lark sends you task notifications, and some arrive as cards with buttons. Tapping Approve or Amend does the real thing: it moves the record to the next stage. Act on them promptly. A card left sitting is work left stuck.

Messaging etiquette · match each role to its channel

One channel per role. Not scattered DMs.

Role
Where updates go
✓ Matched. Every role has a home channel, plus a direct message to the assignee when it is personal.

Keep work updates in the right Updates channel, and direct-message the assignee when it is just for them. Less noise, one source of truth.

Time off and sign-offs
Booking leave? It goes through Approvals.

Time off and approval requests are made in Lark Approvals, not by an informal message to your manager. Submit the request, it routes to the right person, and it is logged. That way your leave is official, visible on the calendar, and never forgotten in a chat thread.

The rule: if it needs a yes from someone, it goes through Approvals. A verbal "sure" in chat is not a record.
Part three

The habits that keep it clean

Do these, avoid those, and understand the why.

Do this · not that

Do ✓

  • Start in your view and work down what is yours
  • Update your own records as work moves
  • Clear every yellow cell before you call it done
  • Act on notification cards promptly
  • Post in the right Updates channel
  • Book leave through Approvals

Don't ✕

  • Message your status instead of updating the record
  • Leave yellow cells empty and move on
  • Ignore cards and let work sit stuck
  • Scatter updates across random direct messages
  • Ask for leave with a casual chat message

One place, kept clean by everyone, is what makes the whole company fast.

Why we do it this way
One place for everything means nothing gets lost.

When work runs through Lark, there is less noise, nothing slips through the cracks, and everyone sees the same truth at the same time. That is the whole point: not more admin, but less chasing, fewer surprises, and a company that can move as one.
