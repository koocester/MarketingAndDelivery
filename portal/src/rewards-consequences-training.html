Rewards &consequences

How we hold the standard here — said out loud, to everyone. The bar is only real if it is rewarded when met and has a consequence when missed.

Why this module exists
Great people stay when the bar is real.

The deal is simple: do great work and you're rewarded for it — coast, and there's a consequence. When that's true and fair, the best people want to stay. When people coast and nothing happens, the best people lose faith and leave.

So we say it out loud, to everyone — what earns a reward, and what happens if you fall short. That plain honesty is exactly what makes the bar fair.
What success looks like
You're winning when…
Everyone knows exactly what earns the next rungthe reward is named, not guessed at
Everyone knows what happens if the bar is missedthe consequence is named too, in advance
It never comes as a surprisesaid at onboarding, in every review, in writing
Reward and consequence are applied fairlythe same bar, the same way, for everyone

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how we hold it — and the final gate checks you against it.

The Koocester frame · abundance, not fear
Performance and belonging are the same thing.

This is not "be tough or be kind" — that's a false trade. People feel they belong because the standard is real and everyone is held to it. A clear reward and a clear consequence are how we say: your work matters, and so does everyone's around you.

The one idea
A bar with no reward and no consequence is just a poster.

The bar — your Key Performance Indicator (KPI) — only becomes real when clearing it earns something and missing it triggers something. And both have to be said out loud, early, and fairly. Said late, or only to some, it isn't a standard — it's a surprise.

Reward is a standard everyone can see, not a mood or a favour: you always know where you stand and what the next climb takes. No politics, no guessing.

Part two

The consequence ladder

The fair, escalating response when the bar is missed — read this as kindness, not punishment.

Sustained under-performance · a fair escalation

Four steps, in order. Most people never leave step one.

1
Coachingthe gap is named, a plan is made, support is given. Most people fix it right here.
2
Performance Improvement Program (PIP)written expectations, real support, a clear timeline, and a defined "what success looks like".
3
Reduced scope or a role changeif the seat is simply wrong for the person.
4
A respectful exitonly if the plan is not met. Done with dignity, no ambush.
This is Empathy & Giving in practice. Escalation is slow, supported, and always aimed first at helping someone succeed — not at removing them.
The reframe · consequence is care
A consequence, said early, is the kindest move there is.
Staying silent "to be nice"
Neglect in a kind costume
the gap grows until it's too big to fix — and the whole team learns the bar is optional
Naming it early, with a plan
Respect, in action
the person gets a real chance to fix it while it's still small — and the bar stays real for everyone

Care is not comfort. The most respectful thing you can do is tell someone the truth early enough that they can still do something about it.

Non-negotiable · no surprises, ever

If it wasn't said, it doesn't count.

Uniting through trust. A consequence a person never saw coming is a failure of ours, not just theirs. It gets said early, said clearly, and put in writing — every time.
Where it all lands · the rhythm
It all ties to the Bi-annual Review.

Twice a year — every six months — we check the same bar for everyone, on the Bi-annual Review Form. Clear it and the reward ladder moves. Miss it repeatedly and the consequence ladder starts — at coaching, not at exit. That six-month rhythm is the heartbeat: predictable, fair, and known to all in advance.

Remember   the reward for a strong six months and the response to a weak one are decided by the same bar, reviewed on the same cadence, for everyone.
