Discovery& qualifying

Running the call that finds a real fit — before anyone wastes time building for a client who was never going to work.

Your mission

Find the fit before you sell the work.

Discovery is one call with one job: understand the client well enough to know if this is a real fit. Qualify hard here, and everything downstream — the deal, the brief, delivery — runs clean.

What success looks like
You're winning when…
You leave knowing if it's a real fita clear yes or no, never a maybe you'll "figure out later"
The deal is loggedcontact, stage and notes in HubSpot before you close the tab
A next step is bookeda concrete action with a date, agreed on the call
Bad fits are passed earlyno time wasted on a client who was never going to work

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you hit it — and the final gate checks you against it.

Why qualify hard
A bad-fit client costs more than no client.

A wrong client drains the delivery team, eats your time, and risks the brand — often while paying the same as a good one. Discovery is the gate that protects delivery. Say no early and you free the team to win with the clients who fit.

Part one

Run the call

Five moves, in order. Rapport to next step.

The discovery call · start to finish

Five moves, in order.

  1. Build rapport — open warm, earn permission to ask real questions.
  2. Understand their goal & situation — where they want to get to, and where they are now.
  3. Surface the real problem & its cost — what's actually blocking them, and what it costs to leave it.
  4. Confirm budget, authority, timeline — money to match, the decision-maker in the room, a realistic date.
  5. Agree the next step — one concrete action, with a date, before you hang up.
The move that separates a pro
Find the real problem. Then price the cost of it.

Anyone can take a brief. Your job is to surface the problem underneath it — and make the cost of not fixing it real. "What is this costing you every month it goes unsolved?" A client who feels the cost is a client ready to buy.

Great questions vs weak questions

Great

open · outcome-focused
  • "How are you currently getting new customers, and what's working or not working for you?"
  • "Which platforms are driving the most enquiries for you right now, and which ones feel like a waste of time?"
  • "Is there a campaign, launch, or season coming up that would make now the right time to get content out?"

Weak

leading · yes / no
  • "Do you do marketing?"
  • "When do you want to start?"
  • "Are you on social media?"
Open questions make them talk. Leading and yes/no questions make them agree without thinking — you learn nothing, and you talk them into a fit that isn't there.
Part two

Read the fit

Four green lights. And the flags that mean walk away.

Fit criteria vs red flags

Real fit →

  • A real growth goal — a specific outcome they want
  • Budget that matches — money to fund the work
  • A decision-maker in the room — the person who can say yes
  • A realistic timeline — a date the work can actually land by

Red flags ✕

  • Chasing vanity metrics — likes and views, no business goal
  • No budget — wants the outcome, won't fund it
  • No decision-maker — has to "check with someone"
  • "Just exploring" — no timeline, no real intent

All four green lights, or it isn't qualified. One red flag isn't always fatal — but two means walk away, however warm they feel.

Log it in HubSpot · the Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system
A lead you don't log is a lead you'll lose.

Before the call is even cold: log the contact, set the deal stage, write your notes, and set the next step with a date. If it isn't in HubSpot, it didn't happen — and the follow-up that lives only in your memory is the one you'll miss.

The skill under the skill
Listen to receive, not to reply.

Most people listen just long enough to load their next line. Don't. Ask the question, then go quiet and actually take in the answer. The person who talks least usually controls the call — because they're the one learning, while the other keeps revealing.
