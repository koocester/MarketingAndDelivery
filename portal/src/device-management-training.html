Company devicesthe do's & don'ts

Your laptop, phone and microphone are company property in your care. This is how you look after them — and what to do the moment something goes wrong.

The frame · read this first
It's not yours. It's in your care.

Every device you're issued — laptop, phone, microphone — is company property. You're the one accountable for it while it's with you: secure, backed up, cared for, and used only for approved work. Treat it better than you'd treat your own.

Accountable, not just holding it. The moment it's assigned to you, its security, its condition and its data are your responsibility.

Part one

Look after it · the DO's

Six habits that keep your device secure and your work safe.

Do · keep it secure
Updated, encrypted, locked.

Install updates when they're offered — they close the security holes attackers use. Keep disk encryption on so a stolen device is useless to a thief. And lock the screen every single time you step away, even for a minute.

Locking is a reflex, not a decision. Stand up, lock it. Coffee, meeting, bathroom — lock it. An unlocked, unattended device is an open door.
Do · back up your work
One copy is no copy.

Keep your work in the company's approved cloud systems, not stranded on the device's desktop. If the only copy of something important lives locally, a spill, a theft or a dead drive erases it forever — and no one can recover it for you.

The test: if your laptop vanished right now, would you lose any work? If the answer is yes, back it up before you do anything else.
Do · keep it safe & cared for
Never leave it unattended in public.

A device out of your sight in a café, a car or a co-working space is a device that walks off. Keep it physically safe, keep it clean and cared for, and protect the screen, the ports and the microphone. Small daily care is what makes it last.

Guard it like cash. You wouldn't leave a stack of notes on a café table while you order — treat the device the same way.
Do · approved software & accounts only
Company work, company tools.

Use only company-approved software and sign in with company accounts for company work. Approved tools are patched, monitored and supported. Random downloads and personal logins are how malware and data leaks get in — and how work ends up somewhere no one can find or protect it.

Unsure if a tool is approved? Ask before you install. A ten-second question beats a security incident.
Part two

Never do this · the DON'Ts

The red lines. Any one of these puts the whole company at risk.

Don't · the red lines

On a company device, never:

Never ✕

  • Install untrusted or pirated software — it's the fastest way to bring in malware
  • Jailbreak or root the device — it strips out the protections that keep it safe
  • Disable security settings — encryption, firewall, screen-lock, updates all stay on
  • Do risky personal browsing on it — sketchy sites and downloads infect the whole machine
  • Let family or friends use it — it's yours to use, no one else's
  • Keep the only copy of important work local — with no backup, one failure loses it all

Every one of these trades a small convenience now for a serious risk to the whole company later. Not worth it.

Personal vs work · keep the line clean

Work lives on the work device.

Do →

  • Keep all company work on the company device
  • Store it in company-approved systems and accounts
  • Sign in to work tools with your work login
  • Keep work reachable, protected and recoverable

Don't ✕

  • Move company work onto a personal laptop or phone
  • Email files to a personal account "to be safe"
  • Save work into personal cloud drives or chats
  • Mix personal logins into company systems
Why it matters: work on personal devices and accounts can't be secured, backed up or handed over cleanly — it's lost the day you're not there.
If something happens · this is the big one
Lost, stolen or broken? Report it now.

The moment a device is lost, stolen or damaged, report it immediately. Speed is everything — a fast report lets us lock it, wipe it remotely and protect the data before anyone can reach it. An hour's delay can turn a minor incident into a real breach.

Reporting early is never punished. Hiding it is the real mistake. Nobody is in trouble for a stolen bag — you're only in trouble for staying quiet while the damage spreads.
Two more standards · offboarding & setup

Return it clean. Flag a bad setup early.

  • Offboarding — when you leave a role or the company, every device and all access is returned cleanly and on time. Nothing walks out the door, nothing stays logged in.
  • Setup standard — if you can't work fast, communicate clearly, and access the systems you need, your setup is not acceptable. Flag it early so it gets fixed — don't quietly struggle on a broken machine.
A bad setup is a bottleneck, not a personality trait. Raise it the moment it slows you down — a slow or half-connected device costs the whole team.
