Part one

Reconciliation

What it is, why it matters, and how you do it.

Reconciliation · what it is and why
Match every transaction to reality.

Reconciliation means matching every transaction — bank ↔ accounting system ↔ card or float — so the books reflect what actually happened. Unreconciled books mean you don't actually know your numbers, no matter how confident the report looks.

How to reconcile · the discipline

Five steps, every time.

  1. Pull the statement — bank, card, or float, for the period.
  2. Match each line — to a record in the accounting system.
  3. Flag the unmatched — anything without a clean pair.
  4. Chase or explain the difference — get the answer, don't guess.
  5. Mark it reconciled — only once every line has a match or an explanation.
Do it on a regular cadence. Weekly or monthly, never let it pile up — a backlog is where errors hide.
How to check the books · the few things to read
Cash positionWhat's actually in the bank right now — not what you think should be there.
P&LMoney in vs money out — is the business actually profitable this period.
ReceivablesWho owes us, and how overdue — chase before it's a write-off.
Runway / burnKnow what's a real source vs an estimate. Don't state a runway you can't back with data.
Part two

Human vs cron

Know exactly which one owns which task.

The split · run the machine, don't do the machine's job

Cron jobs

automate the mundane
  • Pull statements automatically
  • Match obvious, clean transactions
  • Send invoice & payment reminders
  • Run recurring reports
  • Flag duplicates and overdue items

You

own the judgment
  • Review the reconciliation
  • Judge exceptions and odd entries
  • Approvals
  • Month-end sign-off
  • Anything needing a decision or a relationship
The test: if it repeats exactly the same way every time, it's a cron job. If it needs a judgment call, it's yours.
Month-end close · light

Five checks before you call it closed.

  1. Everything reconciled — bank, card, float, all matched.
  2. Receivables & payables current — nothing stale unflagged.
  3. P&L reviewed — the numbers make sense against last month.
  4. Exceptions explained — every variance has an answer attached.
  5. Report out — cash, P&L, and anything odd, in plain language.
Red flags to catch
Trust the numbers only as far as the records are clean.
  • Unexplained variances — a gap with no story behind it.
  • A transaction that doesn't match anything — chase it, don't wave it through.
  • An overdue receivable creeping up — the trend matters more than the single case.
  • A number that "looks off" — trust the instinct enough to check it.
Controls & integrity · brief
  • Separation of duties — whoever spends isn't the one who reconciles.
  • Keep records true — no smoothing, no rounding to make it look tidy.
  • Escalate anything that looks wrong — immediately, not after you've tried to explain it away.
  • Confidentiality — financial data stays need-to-know.
