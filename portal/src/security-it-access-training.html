Security &IT access

The day-one non-negotiables for protecting Koocester's accounts, devices and client data. Everyone does this module — no exceptions.

Why this comes first

One weak login can cost the whole company.

Koocester runs on accounts, devices and client trust. A leaked password, a lost laptop, a clicked phishing link — any one of them can expose client data or hand an attacker the keys. Security isn't the IT team's job. It's everyone's, starting with you.

Part one

Step one: the Declaration

No access is granted until it's signed.

Mandatory under company policy
1The IT & Digital Access Declaration
Sign it before anything else. No Information Technology (IT) or digital access is granted until your signed form is submitted.

It's the form where you accept the rules in this module — how you'll protect accounts, devices and data. It's not paperwork for its own sake: it's the switch that turns your access on. No signed Declaration, no logins.

Do this first. If you haven't submitted the signed Declaration, stop here and ask your manager or IT — everything after this assumes it's done.
Part two

Lock down your accounts

Passwords, two-factor, and never sharing.

Account hygiene · the four habits

Make every login hard to break.

  1. Strong, unique passwords — a different one for every account, generated and stored in the company password manager. Never reuse, never write them down.
  2. Two-factor authentication (2FA) on everything — a second step beyond the password, on every account that offers it. It stops a stolen password on its own.
  3. Never share credentials — not with a colleague, not "just this once". Access is granted to you, and it stays with you.
  4. Lock your screen — every time you step away, even for a minute. An unlocked laptop is an open door.
Bitwarden · our password manager

Bitwarden holds every password — so you don't have to.

Bitwarden is the company vault. It generates strong, unique passwords, stores them safely, and fills them in for you. It's how we keep every login hard to break — with zero effort to remember.

  • One strong password per account — always let Bitwarden generate and store it. Reused or weak passwords stay in the past.
  • Credentials live in Bitwarden only — always keep passwords in the vault, never in chats, emails, or on a sticky note.
  • Shared logins are shared through Bitwarden — the secure way to hand someone a page or social login, always.
Who holds the shared page & social logins: only Producers, Social Media Managers, and the Marketing Coordinator — the people who post to and manage the pages. Tech administers the vault and controls who can see what. Everyone else still uses Bitwarden every day for their own strong passwords.
Device security · your laptop and phone

A safe device is updated, encrypted, and clean.

  • Keep it updated — install operating system and app updates promptly. Most attacks exploit old, unpatched software.
  • Keep it encrypted — turn on full-disk encryption so a lost device doesn't mean leaked data.
  • Don't install untrusted software — no random apps, extensions, or downloads. If you're not sure it's safe, ask IT before you install.
  • Report a lost or stolen device immediately — the faster IT knows, the faster access can be cut and the device wiped.
Data & confidentiality · the one to internalise above all
Critical — this is the big one
Client data never leaves approved systems.

This is the single most important rule in this module. Company and client data stays inside the tools we've approved for it — always. Nothing gets shared, downloaded, or sent externally without permission. And treat what you paste into outside tools with real care — including artificial intelligence (AI) tools: once it's pasted, you've sent it out, and you can't take it back.

The simple test: if you're about to move data outside an approved system — email, download, upload, paste — stop and ask whether you have permission. If you're not sure, you don't. Get this one right every single time.
Part three

Spotting the trap

Phishing, links, and when to slow down.

Phishing & links · treat the unexpected as suspicious

When in doubt, don't click — verify.

Red flags ✕

  • An unexpected link or attachment, even from a known name
  • Urgent pressure: "act now", "your account will be closed"
  • A login page reached from a link in an email or message
  • A sender address that's slightly off, or doesn't match

Do this instead →

  • Verify the sender through a channel you trust
  • Go to the site directly, don't follow the link
  • Hover to see the real destination before anything
  • When unsure, ask IT before you click

There's no penalty for checking. There's a real cost to clicking a bad link. Slow down — it takes seconds.

Scam & fake emails · treat the unexpected as suspicious
Everyone — especially page & email handlers

Real platforms never email you to "confirm your password".

Common fakes to distrust ✕

  • "You're verified" / "your account is verified" out of nowhere
  • Fake Facebook, Meta or Instagram emails about your page
  • "Confirm your account" or "login required" links
  • Unexpected invoices or payment requests

The tells — always check →

  • Read the real sender address, not just the display name
  • Hover a link to see where it truly goes before clicking
  • Platforms never ask you to confirm a password by email
  • Verify inside the real app — never through the email's link

This is for everyone, but doubly so if you handle email or the pages — Sales in the inbox, Social Media Managers on the accounts. When a "verification" or "urgent account" email lands, open the real app yourself and confirm there.

Access = responsibility

What happens under your login is on you.

  • You're accountable — activity under your account is treated as yours. Guard it like it is.
  • Least privilege — you get only the access you need to do your job, nothing more. If you're offered access you don't need, decline it.
  • Off-board cleanly — when you leave a project or the company, access is handed back and closed out. No lingering logins.
More access isn't a perk. Every extra login you hold is another door someone could come through. Keep your footprint small.
If something goes wrong · speed is everything
Critical — report fast, always
Report it fast. Hiding it is the only real mistake.

Clicked a bad link? Lost a device? Sent something to the wrong place? Tell IT or your manager immediately. There is no blame for honest, early reporting — a problem caught in minutes is small; one hidden for a day can become a breach. This is one of the most important habits in the whole module.

Speed beats shame every time. The people who report fast protect the company. The only unforgivable move is staying quiet and hoping.
