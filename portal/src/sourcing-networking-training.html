Sourcing &networking

Finding credible people worth featuring, building a real relationship, and locking the shoot. Networking is the skill underneath all of it.

How to win

A qualified, credible candidate, confirmed in writing, shoot date locked.

Qualified means they pass all five questions in the qualification screen, even one "No" kills it. And underneath the booking sits the real win: a genuine relationship with someone worth knowing.

What success looks like
You're winning when…
Credible people foundreal, verifiable, and worth featuring
A real relationship builtnot just a booking, someone glad they met you
All 5 gates passeven one "No" and the candidate is out
Shoot locked in writingthe date confirmed, never just a verbal yes

This is the bar. Everything that follows is how you hit it — and the final gate checks you against it.

Why it matters
Sourcing is the biggest driver of credibility, growth, and virality.

A great candidate carries average production. A weak candidate sinks great production. We never ship a video without a qualified source, so the person you find matters more than anything you shoot.

Three rules that never bend
All 5 questions pass
Every qualifying question must be a Yes. Even one "No" kills the candidate.
Never promise payment
Exposure is the value exchange. Mentioning compensation cheapens the brand.
Networking is the trait
"I can't find anyone" is a gap in resourcefulness, not the market.
Part one

Networking

The relationship, before the ask.

What networking success looks like

You have networked well with a high-net-worth person when all four are true:

✓They accept you as a friend or connection
✓They reply warmly and are open to staying in touch
✓They remember you at the next interaction
✓The relationship later produces a warm intro or referral
The comfortable impression is the goal. Not a booking on day one, a person who is glad they met you.
How to leave a comfortable impression

Do

  • Lead with genuine interest, ask about something they actually said or built
  • Be specific, not generic flattery
  • Give before you take, be useful or interesting first
  • Be people-smart, read the room and match their energy
  • Add them as a contact and follow up lightly

Don't

  • Lead with the ask, no pitch in the first minute
  • Mention payment, ever
  • Over-talk, listen more than you speak
  • Force it, a comfortable no beats a pushy yes
Where to source · channels in priority
1Events highest signal, you've met them in person
2Warm referrals from your existing network
3LinkedIn founders, professionals, B2B
4Instagram foodie, homes, lifestyle
5Publications, podcasts, articles
6Customer recommendations & existing networks
Part two

Outreach

Reach out, follow up, handle objections.

Reach out · pre-send checklist
  • A specific reference to something they actually said, built, or won, never generic flattery.
  • Their name spelled correctly.
  • Channel-appropriate length: DM 60 words or less, email 120 words or less.
Templates live in the wiki (warm intro, in-person at an event, cold DM, cold email). They are starters, be people-smart and adjust for the situation.
Follow-up rules
  • After a cold DM or email: wait 4 working days, follow up exactly once. Maximum 2 touchpoints from a cold start.
  • After that: park them, re-engage in 6 months with a different angle.
  • After a verbal yes but no date locked: re-engage at 7 days, mark Lost at 14 days.
Handling objections

Acknowledge, diagnose, reframe, soft close.

1 · Acknowledge
"Totally understand." "That's fair." "Makes sense."
2 · Diagnose
"What part are you most unsure about, the exposure, timing, topic, or being on camera?"
3 · Reframe
Bring it back to story, value, credibility, or controlled positioning.
4 · Soft close
"If we keep it aligned with that, would you be open to exploring?"
Part three

Qualify, verify, lock

Credible, confirmed, in writing.

Qualify · the five gates
Five Yeses, or it's a No.
  1. Real & verifiable — is their success genuine, and does it survive an external check (registry, company footprint)?
  2. Clean reputation — does the market vouch for them? A quiet network check comes back with no red flags.
  3. Right voice for the Page — are they a true fit for this vertical and audience, not a stretch?
  4. A story worth watching — do they carry a story or insight that can travel and earn the view?
  5. Exposure-aligned & camera-ready — happy to feature for earned exposure (no payment) and comfortable on camera?
Every answer must be Yes. Even one "No" kills the candidate — no exceptions.
Verify externally · never interrogate
  • Business registry check — confirm the company exists, they are a director or owner, dates align (e.g. ACRA).
  • Reverse company search — LinkedIn employee count, customer case studies, news footprint. Real businesses leave traces, shell brands have one website and a TikTok.
  • Quiet network check — DM one or two trusted contacts in their space: "Ever come across [Name]? How are they?"
Rule: if two or more come back weak, drop the candidate. Don't book.
Lock & schedule

Send the booking confirmation covering the date, location, time and duration, what we'll cover, what to bring, and a reaffirmation, no payment, earned exposure.

Get it in writing. A verbal yes is not a lock, the candidate must reply confirming the date.

Random shoots (captured first, qualified after): upload to the Tracker as DO NOT POST, a private record flagged out of the publish queue. The Senior Producer revisits these periodically.
