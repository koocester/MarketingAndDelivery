You decide what ships
You are the gate.

The Content strategist holds source approval and QC. The Marketing coordinator holds final approval and organic approval. Nothing goes live without one of you. Judge to a standard, not a mood, and the whole pipeline rises to it.

The decision

Approve, or send it back.

Approve →

  • Clears every check, no exceptions
  • Moves forward, clock passes to the next owner
  • No "approved but…", if it needs a change, it's not approved

Send back ✕

  • Misses at least one check
  • Rejected into the fix-and-resubmit view, with a clear reason
  • Amendment clock starts the moment it lands there
Amendment strategies

Feedback that gets fixed fast.

  • Be specific. Comment on the frame, name the exact change. Never "make it better."
  • Match the video's language. Write the amendment in the same language used in the video.
  • Point to the standard. Cite the storyboard or brand guideline, not your opinion.
  • Must-fix first. List what blocks approval, then the nice-to-haves.
  • One round. Send the whole list at once, not one fix at a time.
Vague vs specific · commenting in Frame.io

Comment on the frame. Say the exact change.

Vague · slow to fix

"The intro feels off, can you fix the hook and tighten it up a bit?"

Specific · fixed fast

"Open on the price reveal, not the walk-in."  ·  "Dead air here — cut it."  ·  "Caption breaks brand case — lower-case it."

Frame.io stamps the timecode for you. Pause on the exact frame and name the precise change. Always write the amendment in the same language used in the video.
Coach the person, not just the piece
One miss is a fix. A repeat is a pattern.

Reject the video and you fix one clip. Spot the same check failing again and again and you've found the real problem — the skill, not the piece. That's the yellow-card conversation. Coach it once, and every video after it gets better.

How we say it
Reframe every "no" into "here's what works".

Amendments raise the standard, they do not knock the person down. Lead with what to do, not what is wrong. Specific, direct, and from abundance, that is how feedback lands and the work gets better fast.
