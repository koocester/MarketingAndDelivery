Why you're here
You're a player-coach now.

Part of your week is still doing the work. The other part is leading it. Keep only the work only you can do — everything else is now a lever, not a task. Your real output is no longer what you personally produce. It's what your team produces.

The one shift: fewer hours doing, more hours making your team better.
Where your hours go

Spend your time where only you can.

High-leverage → this is you

  • Set priorities and direction
  • A focused 1-on-1
  • Feedback and coaching
  • Train once — it saves twenty hours later
  • Unblock the team
  • Make the decision they're waiting on

Low-leverage → hand it off

  • Redoing their work
  • Tasks anyone on the team could do
  • Meetings you're not needed in
  • Weekly firefighting

The job, in four duties

What a manager actually owns.

  1. Set direction — the team knows what matters this week and why.
  2. Grow people — coach, don't rescue.
  3. Drive outcomes — numbers over busyness.
  4. Hold the standard — it's kept by what you tolerate.

Your job starts the moment a record stops moving.

Guard the lead times

The daily read, in order.

  1. What's overdue? Anything past its clock is a dropped ball — chase it first.
  2. Where's the wall? The stage with the most records piled up is today's bottleneck.
  3. Who's overloaded? Rebalance the queue before someone drowns.
The gates are deliberate. Every stage change is a button someone presses — that's what stops half-baked work moving forward. Your job is making sure the right person presses it, on time.
When it's stuck

Chase the person, not the record.

  • Overdue at a gate? The approver is the blocker. Go to them directly, today.
  • Bouncing in amendments? The notes weren't specific enough — get one clear pass.
  • Aging in Ready to Upload? That's a backlog, not a mystery — rebalance or reprioritise.

This is separate from escalating people matters to Human Resources — this is you, running the floor.

The 4 basics · 1 of 4
Set crystal-clear expectations.
  • Outcomes, not tasks. Tell them what "done well" produces, not just what to do.
  • Define what good looks like — show an example.
  • Always give the why.
  • Agree, then write it down.

Most performance problems are clarity problems.

The weekly 1-on-1.

It's their meeting, not your status update.

1
How are you?
2
What's working or stuck?
3
Feedback both ways
4
Growth & next step

Default: weekly, 30 minutes, every direct report — on video across Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia. Never skip it.

How you run it
Listening isn't just words.

A great 1-on-1 runs on three moves: you listen for the pattern, you ask the open question, then you get out of the way.

Listen for

  • Hesitation before a "yes"
  • What they don't say
  • Energy dropping on a topic
  • Repeating the same worry
  • Over-explaining
  • "I'm fine" that isn't

Ask

  • "What's really going on?"
  • "What's the hard part?"
  • "What do you need from me?"
  • "What am I missing?"

They speak

  • Talking 70%, you 30%
  • They surface the real issue
  • You guide, you don't lead the witness
How you communicate

Do this. Steer clear of that.

Do

  • Listen first
  • Be specific
  • Ask, don't assume
  • Explain why
  • Address behaviour, not personality
  • Praise in public, coach in private
  • Stay calm
  • Confirm understanding

Steer clear

  • Interrupting
  • ⚠ Assuming intentcommon trap — easy to default to, must catch yourself
  • ⚠ Vague feedbackcommon trap — specific is harder, do it anyway
  • Public embarrassment
  • ⚠ Speaking from emotioncommon trap — pause before you react
  • Empty promises
  • ⚠ Avoiding hard talkscommon trap — hardest to avoid, most costly to skip

Standard: Clear · Respectful · Honest · Timely.

The 4 basics · 3 of 4
Feedback, direct and kind.
  1. Situation — when and where it happened.
  2. Behaviour — exactly what you observed.
  3. Impact — the effect it had.
  4. Forward — what happens next.

Care personally, challenge directly. Praise in public, correct in private.

Coach, don't rescue

The GROW shape.

  1. Understand — what's really going on.
  2. Explore — the options with them, not for them.
  3. Ownership — they choose the path.
  4. Support — you back them, you don't take over.
Don't solve every problem for them — or you become the bottleneck.

When performance falls short

Work the gap, in order.

  1. Clarify expectations — make sure "good" was ever actually defined.
  2. Discuss the gap — facts, not feelings.
  3. Understand the cause — skill, motivation, resources, or clarity.
  4. Agree improvement actions.
  5. Follow up.

Early beats formal.

The hard part · accountability
The hard thing goes first.

Fast. Every time. Never deferred. As a leader, the hard — and the heart — things are always addressed first, immediately, ahead of everything else on your list.

Not eventually. Not when it's convenient. First.
  • Name it early.
  • Name it without softening.
  • Separate the person from the problem.
  • Agree the fix, and the date.

The kindest thing is to be honest early.

Having the conversation

Care first. Then the truth.

  1. Open with care — "I'm telling you this because I back you."
  2. State the gap plainly.
  3. Listen, really.
  4. Land on forward — and the next check-in.
Leading across Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia

Remote and hybrid.

  • Communicate clearly — nothing left to guess.
  • Stay connected — weekly 1-on-1s, video when it matters.
  • Create visibility — key performance indicators, dashboards, not vibes.
  • Build trust — deliberately, it doesn't happen by accident remotely.

Presence is not the same as productivity.

Know the line

What you own. What you escalate.

You own

  • Setting expectations
  • Giving feedback
  • Coaching
  • Documenting the facts
  • Raising concerns early

Escalate to Human Resources

  • Harassment
  • Misconduct
  • Disciplinary action
  • Medical matters
  • Grievances

Never promise promotions, raises, or guarantees — that's not yours to give.
