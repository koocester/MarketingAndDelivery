Storytelling &showmanship

For Producer, Content strategist, Copywriter and Marketing coordinator. This isn't the storyboard template — it's the skill underneath it: making people care, and stay.

What success looks like
You're winning when…
They watch to the endno one drops off before the payoff
They feel somethingcuriosity, tension, or a lump in the throat
A stranger remembers it tomorrowthe story outlives the scroll
The number lands as meaningnot a flex — a lesson they carry

Part one

The art of holding attention

What actually makes people care, and stay.

The one idea
People don't remember information. They remember story — and how you made them feel.

Showmanship is earning attention and holding it. Facts fade. A story sticks because it moves someone, and movement is what people carry with them and repeat to someone else.

Curiosity gap
Open a loop the viewer needs closed.

Tension is what keeps them watching. The gap between what they know and what they want to know is the entire reason they stay to the end — resolve it too early, and they leave.

Test it: if you can guess the ending from the hook, the gap isn't open yet. Rewrite until they have to keep watching to find out.
Show, don't tell
A specific scene beats an abstract claim.

Specifics let people SEE it. The vaguer the claim, the less real it feels — and the less real it feels, the less they care.

Tell (abstract)

  • "Your car"
  • "A nice house"
  • "A lot of money"

Show (specific)

  • "This Ferrari"
  • "The house with the infinity pool over the bay"
  • "$10M hitting the account at 2am"
Make the data a story, never a flex
Pull authority through a lesson, never a raw stat.

"$10M revenue" isn't the story. "What changed about how you run the business" is. A number alone brags — a number wrapped in a lesson teaches, and teaching is what earns trust.

The rewrite: $10M revenue → "what changed about how you run the business?" Same fact. One is a flex. One is educational.
The emotional arc

Every good piece moves the viewer.

Curiosity→ Tension→ Payoff→ Meaning

Make them feel before you make them think. If a piece is flat — no rise, no release — the information can be perfect and it will still fail to land.

Showmanship & delivery

Energy earns attention.

  • Vary pace — slow on the punchline, fast on the setup.
  • Land the number — pause before it, pause after it.
  • Vocal smile — warmth carries even when they can't see you.
  • Conversational, not presentational — never monotone.
  • The "wow" only lands when it's earned — build to it, don't announce it.
Make one person universal
The viewer has to see themselves in the story.

"That's me" or "that's what I want" — tie the specific detail to the viewer's own life. Without that thread, the specific stays someone else's story forever, and they scroll past it as someone else's win.

The save-worthy line
Every piece needs one line worth saving or sending.

Quotable. Screenshot-able. That's what makes content travel past the person who first watched it — they become the distribution, for free.

Before you ship: point to the one line in this piece someone would screenshot. If you can't point to it, it doesn't exist yet — write it.
Part two

Where this shows up

Same skill, a different lever per role.

Same skill, six roles

Where this shows up per role.

Producer

Coach the guest to tell it well; improvise the burning follow-up the viewer would shout.

Content strategist

Build the arc. Build the tension.

Copywriter

The hook, the quotable line, the caption that travels.

Marketing coordinator

Every video earns attention and drives the outcome.

Social media manager

The caption and the first line of every post is a story hook; community replies that make people feel seen.

Sales

The pitch IS a story — sell the transformation, not features. A story closes where a feature list stalls.

Part three

Your turn

Four calls. Get them graded.

Question 1 · authority via story vs. stat
You're writing a caption about a founder who hit $10M revenue this year.
What earns more trust in the caption?
Question 2 · curiosity gap
The hook says: "Watch what happens when a guest tries to negotiate a $50 wagyu steak down to $20."
Why does this hook work?
Question 3 · show, don't tell
Two captions for the same video: "He drives a nice car" vs. "He drives a Ferrari 812 Superfast, one of eleven in the country."
Which is stronger, and why?
Question 4 · the save-worthy line
You've finished a caption with a strong arc, but nothing anyone would screenshot.
What does this piece lack?
