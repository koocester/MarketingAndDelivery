The base layer everyone stands on. Where we talk, how we talk, when we meet — and the one room that keeps us honest.

Why this is the foundation

Great work moves at the speed of clear communication.

Every role, every project, every ritual runs on the same base layer: we talk in the right place, we always carry an ask, and we meet only to decide. Get this right and everything downstream gets faster.

Part one

Where we talk

Two channels. Each has a job. Don't mix them up.

Two channels, two jobs

Lark

the system of record
  • Tasks — the work lives here
  • Protocols — our standard way of doing things
  • Project management — every project, tracked
  • Daily huddles, by department

WhatsApp

external & fast
  • Talking to people outside the company
  • Quick, time-sensitive back-and-forth
  • Never where a task or decision is logged
The rule: if it's work, it's in Lark. If it's fast or external, WhatsApp. A decision made on WhatsApp isn't real until it lands as a Lark task.
Set Lark up right · once, today
Two settings. Do them now.
  1. Open links in Navigation Bar — so links open cleanly inside Lark instead of bouncing you out. Turn it on in your Lark settings.
  2. Set your timezone — critical when you're travelling. If your timezone is wrong, every deadline and huddle time reads wrong. Fix it before you fly.
Why it matters: the whole company runs on Lark's clock. A wrong timezone quietly makes you late to everything — and nobody can see why.
Part two

How we work

Five principles. They apply to everyone, everywhere.

The five principles
  1. Tasks must have an owner and a deadline — one name, one date. No orphans.
  2. Meetings must create action items — if nothing's assigned, the meeting didn't happen.
  3. No task = no work — if it isn't a task in Lark, it doesn't exist and won't get done.
  4. Speed > perfection — ship, learn, improve. A good move now beats a perfect one later.
  5. Clarity > assumptions — spell it out. Never assume the other person filled the gap the way you would.

The one that changes everything
A message with no ask is noise.

Before you hit send, ask yourself: what do I want the reader to do? If there's no answer, you're adding to the pile, not moving work forward. Lead with the point, name the ask, give the deadline. Every time.

Part four

When we meet

The rhythm of the company. Know what each ritual is for.

The meeting & ritual cadence
Leadership / dept syncTBCSet the week. Leadership and departments align on priorities and what must land next.
Individual 1-on-1TBCYour coaching check-in. One-to-one with your manager — progress, blockers, growth.
Group 1-to-1TBC“The truth room.” A small group shares what's really going on. The culture jewel — more on the next slides.
Department closeTBCClose the week. Department reviews what shipped, what slipped, and hands off cleanly.
Team bondingTBCBuild the relationships the work rides on. We're a team before we're a team of roles.
Why TBC? With the new dashboards and live data, the picture is visible without a meeting — so routine meetings are becoming lighter and less frequent. The rituals stay; we're finalising exactly how often each one runs. Async-first, meet only when it earns it.
Part five · the culture jewel

The truth room

Once a month. Five people. Total honesty.

The monthly Group 1-to-1 · three things, in turn

Win

what went right
  • Something real you're proud of this month
  • Name it out loud — belonging starts here

Struggle

what costs you to say
  • A real struggle, not a safe one
  • If it doesn't cost something to say it, go deeper

Need

a specific ask
  • A concrete request from one named person
  • Not “help” — a clear, answerable ask
Roughly five people. Each person shares all three, in turn. You listen to receive, not to reply — no fixing, no rebutting, just hearing.
Why the truth room works
Performance and belonging are the same thing.

Not a trade-off, not a balance — the same thing. People do their best work where they feel they belong, and they belong where they can tell the truth. “What's said here stays here.” That confidentiality is what makes the honesty possible.
