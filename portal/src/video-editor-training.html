Part one

The look

Fonts, the caption colour code, and subtitle specs.

Typography · three fonts, one system

Know which font, and when.

Helvetica PrimaryDefault for everything — subtitles, names, company names, locations, most on-screen text.
Inter Black SecondaryBig, bold emphasis — prices, restaurant / car names, the punchy hero lines.
Times New Roman Secondary · italicReserved for the role / title label under a person's name (interviewee's role). Always italic.
The caption colour code · learn it cold

Colour tells the viewer who's speaking.

These are the subtitle colours you apply as content — not the deck's colours. Red Green Blue (RGB) hex values:

Interviewerthe person asking / narrating#ffffff
Intervieweethe guest answering#ffcb00
Additional / positivethe highlight, the good news#00ff18
Negative statementthe setback, the tension#ec1d1d
Subtitle details · set them exactly

Same numbers, every time.

SettingValueWhy
Font size7 baseIncrease only when emphasis or readability needs it
Character spacing−1Tighter, cleaner, more premium
Shadow colourBlackLifts text off any background
Shadow opacity60%Present but not heavy
Shadow blur10%Soft edge, no hard line
Shadow distance8Enough separation to read
Shadow angle−45°Consistent light direction
Subtitle checklist: start at size 7, then scale up selectively for emphasis. Keep every line typo-free, synced, on-brand and inside the safe area.

Part three

Motion & sound

Text animation, audio, sound effects, assets.

Text animation · interviewer vs interviewee

Move it right, cut it clean.

Interviewer

the question / narration
  • Slide-up animation in
  • Cut after every phrase
  • Size & placement adjustable
  • Helvetica white — keep interviewer text white throughout

Interviewee

the answer
  • Cut after every phrase
  • Helvetica yellow #ffcb00
  • Slide-up + Inter Black green line 2 for key statements
  • Size & placement adjustable
Ka-ching & b-roll: money reveals slide up then fade out (Line 1 Helvetica white, Line 2 Inter Black green + glow). Extra b-roll stays futuristic, simple Canva animation.
Sound · raw audio, effects, assets
Raw footage audioVolume set to max. Fade in and fade out both 0.0s. Keep the guest's voice clean and present.
Sound effectsUse the assets in the CapCut Space Library. Match each effect to the video's tone — serious, professional or entertaining — then balance it so it supports the cut without overpowering it.
Additional b-rollFuturistic look, simple animation built in Canva. Supports the story, never distracts.
Endings & brand marks

Call to action (CTA)

  • Follow the lead magnet set by the Content Strategist and Marketing Team
  • Show the automation trigger word, such as RENOVATION or MODIF
  • Place it in the middle or at the end, wherever the story makes the action feel natural

Watermark

  • Use the fixed watermark provided in the CapCut space
  • Apply from the first second to the end
  • Fixed position — strictly no adjustments

The workflow

How the work moves, and the standard at each stage.

Your edit kit

Editing workflow · the stage order

Rough cut to render, in order.

  1. Rough cut — fast-paced, well-structured story, strong hook, easy to follow.
  2. Subtitle — no typos, clear colours, synced timing, inside the safe area.
  3. Recheck 1 — typos, subtitle timing, audio, watermark, CTA, frames, motion, transitions, exports.
  4. Motion — smooth, on-brand, supports the story, never distracting.
  5. SFX — supports emotion, balanced, timed to the pacing.
  6. Render draft 1 → Quality Check (AI Amender Bot) → Render final.
Quality Check: upload to the AI Amender Bot, review the recommendations, apply only the relevant revisions — before it goes to Frame.io.

Delivery · upload & amendments

Uploading

  • Upload final to Frame.io in the correct folder: Country → Year → Pages → Month → Paid/Unpaid
  • Copy & submit the Frame.io link into the Lark form
  • Attach the exported TXT transcript in the Video TXT (Transcript) column of the same form — not Frame.io anymore
  • Inform the respective Producer for review

Amendments

  • Check for incoming amendments every morning, prioritise them before new projects
  • Upload revisions to "To Review" named V1, V2, V3…
  • Move previous versions to "Previous Draft", inform the Producer, repeat until finalised
Delivery · which link you send

Reviewers get the Share link. Always.

When you send a video to anyone for review or approval, always send the Frame.io Share link, never the normal project link. Same video, two very different doors.

Normal link · keep it internal

app.frame.io/…
  • The app URL copied from your project workspace
  • Often needs a Frame.io login or workspace access
  • Not built for outsiders, so a Producer, client or guest can hit a wall

Share link · send this one

f.io/… · the "Share for review" URL
  • Made for reviewers: opens the player with comments
  • No workspace access needed and no login barrier
  • You control the permissions on what they can see
The rule, one line: reviewers get the Share link. Copy it from the Share button, never from the address bar.
Hard no · things editors are not allowed to do

Do any of these and it's on you.

  • Missing deadlines without notice, or ignoring amendments and feedback
  • Uploading videos without a Quality Check, or leaving typo, audio or subtitle mistakes
  • Using incorrect export settings, or poor file organisation and naming
  • Ignoring or changing brand guidelines without approval
  • Sharing confidential files or footage — or a face reveal
