Your mission

The sale isn't done at "yes". It's done on a clean bridge.

Two moves. First, close — ask for the obvious yes. Second, build the bridge — pass delivery a brief so complete they never have to call you for missing information. Both, every time.

Part one

Closing the client

If discovery was done well, the close is easy.

Closing is not a trick
The close is asking for the obvious yes.

If you did discovery well, you already know their goal, their problem, and what they want. The close isn't pressure or a clever line — it's simply naming the obvious next step and inviting them to take it.

The close · say it back, then ask

Summarise, then invite the yes.

1 · Their goal
"You want to…"
→
2 · The outcome
"Here's what we'll deliver"
→
3 · The offer
"For this scope and price"
→
4 · The ask
"Shall we begin?"
Mirror their own words back. Goal → outcome → offer → "shall we begin?". When they hear their goal repeated and matched to a clear outcome, the yes is the natural answer.
Part two

Handling objections

Never argue. Never hedge. Reframe.

The three you'll hear · reframe every one

Price

"It's too expensive"
  • The value isn't clear yet
  • Re-anchor on the outcome and the cost of not acting

Timing

"Not right now"
  • Name the cost of waiting
  • Show how starting now brings the result forward

Trust

"Will this work for us?"
  • Bring proof — results, track record, a guarantee
  • Make the first step small and safe
Reframe every objection into "here's what works." You don't fight the objection and you don't hedge — you turn it into the reason to move.
The price objection · what it really means
"Too expensive" means the value isn't clear yet.

It's almost never really about the number. Don't discount and don't defend the price — re-anchor on the outcome they'll get and the cost of doing nothing. When the value is obvious, the price stops being the question.

Re-anchor, don't drop. "For the result you want, here's what it's worth — and here's what staying stuck keeps costing you."
Part three

The clean bridge

The moment they say yes, delivery inherits the work.

Marketing & Delivery base
the work starts right

The Marketing & Delivery base is where the work gets made. Your brief is the bridge to it. A clean brief means delivery starts the moment they inherit — no waiting, no guessing.

A clean brief · six things, every time

What delivery needs to start right

  1. The client and their goal — who they are and what they came for.
  2. The offer sold — exactly what they bought.
  3. Deliverables and scope — what's in, what's out.
  4. Timeline — key dates and the deadline.
  5. Key contacts — who delivery talks to on the client side.
  6. Anything promised on the call — every extra you committed to.
A vague bridge creates rework and broken promises

Clean vs vague

Clean brief →

  • Delivery starts same day, no questions
  • Scope and deadline are unambiguous
  • Everything promised is written down
  • The client gets exactly what they were sold

Vague bridge ✕

  • Delivery has to call you to start
  • Scope gets guessed — rework follows
  • A promise you made never lands
  • The client feels the drop between sales and delivery
Before you leave the call

Log the win. Build the bridge. Set expectations.

  • Log the won deal in HubSpot — never let a won client sit in an inbox. The record starts the bridge.
  • Build the bridge — the brief goes to delivery the moment the deal is marked won, not "later".
  • Tell them what happens next — the steps from here, in plain terms.
  • Name who they'll hear from, and when — so the client is never left wondering.
A won client in an inbox is a client half-lost. Log it, bridge it, and set expectations — all before you hang up.
