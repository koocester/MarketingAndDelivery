Part one

In the base

What you fill, and where you look.

Your view

The sourcing queue is home.

The base is the team's shared tracker in Lark. Your view inside it, Producer Sourcing, lists every video waiting for you to find and confirm a guest. Check it daily.

When you source a guest, you fill
Guest contact Guest name IG handle Business handle Profession Number

Sourcing means finding and confirming the guest for a video. Fill these on the video's record, then press Confirm submission so marketing can approve before you book the shoot.

After the shoot
Shoot done Raw file link (Google Drive)

The raw file link is the Google Drive link to your unedited footage. Paste it in within 16 calendar hours of the shoot or the base flags you overdue. Don't sit on footage.

The whole operational discipline
Fill the yellow. Confirm the guest. Raw in 16h.

A bright-yellow cell means must fill. That's it. Do those three, every time.

Part two

The habit

The one thing that makes you great.

The always on backlog

Never let a good one slip.

The second you meet someone worth featuring, capture them in 20 seconds: name, contact, which vertical they fit (homes, business, food, autos, wealth), one line why. You are always feeding the pipeline.

Every shoot wrap
"Who did I meet today? Add them."

The people you meet this month are next month's shoots. A full backlog turns planning into picking from a shelf.

Part three

Sourcing

Being featured is the reward. You offer, you don't beg.

Channels, best first
  1. Warm referrals. Ask every guest: "Who else should we feature?"
  2. Feature first. "We'd love to feature you", never "can I interview you".
  3. Content as a gift. Offer a business something valuable. They say yes and promote it.
  4. LinkedIn. Founders. One specific reason they fit.
  5. Instagram. Homes, autos, foodie. Engage first, then offer.
The multiplier

Every feature recruits the next.

A published feature tags the guest. They reshare. Their network becomes your next guests. Design every shoot to earn the next referral.

Part four

The pitch

Lead with them. Be specific. Make the yes easy.

The pitch

"Hi [name], I'm with Koocester. We feature [vertical] stories our audience loves, and your [specific thing] stood out. We come to you, it's about an hour, and you get a polished piece to share. Open to it?"

Leads with them. Names something specific. Makes the yes low cost.

On the shoot
  • Arrive early, look professional, put the guest at ease.
  • Direct them warmly. They don't know what to do, you do.
  • Clean audio above all. Cover every shot on the storyboard, the shot plan written before your shoot, plus extra B roll.
  • Before you leave: confirm handles, ask for the next referral, thank them.
Getting started

Your first day

  1. Open the base and find the Producer Sourcing view. That queue is yours.
  2. Open one video marked Sourcing needed and read the idea and the page it serves.
  3. Find the guest fields you own: name, handles, number, profession.
  4. Practice the pitch script once out loud.
  5. When a guest says yes, fill the contact and press submit so marketing can approve.
  6. Check the Producer Updates channel. The base talks to you there.
